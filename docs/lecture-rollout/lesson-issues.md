# What the lecture writers found in the lessons

Writing a lecture script means checking it line by line against its lesson, so the writers noticed places where a lesson contradicts itself. No lesson was changed. Each line below is a place for an editor to look.

## score-the-vote-book
- what-is-score-voting.mdx: prose says Avila and Chen each have "two detractors who scored them at the floor", but the table gives Avila 0, 1, 0 and Chen 1, 0, 2. (Script says "at or near the floor".)
- what-is-score-voting.mdx: the figure says the highest city AVERAGE wins; the prose says highest TOTAL. (Script uses totals.)
- introduction.mdx: body says 64 percent for Fargo; its source note says 63.5.
- introduction.mdx: "Removed from above" sits beside a ban passed by Missouri VOTERS.
- introduction / the-plurality-problem / what-is-score-voting: frontmatter names a "Status-quo defender" perspective, but none of the three gives it an argument (and none has PerspectiveTabs).

## congress
- 01-two-chambers.mdx ends "Next in this pack: who actually wins… seats", but the pack has one lesson.
- 01-two-chambers.mdx frontmatter says Federalist 62 is "Hamilton or Madison"; the body says Madison.

## three-branches
- 02-checks-and-balances.mdx narrates the 1975 Train ruling, then says "Congress passed" the 1974 Act: reads out of order.

## three-branches-2
- 02-how-one-branch-stops-another.mdx: the body never says Bork and Garland were Supreme Court nominees (only its sources do).

## voter-id-and-mail-ballots
- 01-how-it-works.mdx: prose says 23 photo-ID states; its table sums to 24 (10 strict + 14 non-strict).
- 03-the-policy-landscape.mdx: 53 Republican seats, Murkowski the lone Republican no, yet 51 yes votes (the arithmetic does not close).
- 03-the-policy-landscape.mdx: the water-rule distance differs between the table and the checkpoint.

## how-bills-become-law-2
- 01-why-most-bills-die.mdx lists "One text" and "The President" under "The veto points Congress built", though its own text makes them constitutional.
- 01-why-most-bills-die.mdx gives no year for the Constitution; Hamilton's 1788 is only in the frontmatter. 02 never gives Hamilton's first name.

## bill-of-rights-2
- 01-who-it-protects-you-from.mdx: the figure caption says half the amendments concern investigating, trying and punishing (Fourth to Eighth); the prose puts the weight on "the third group" (Fifth to Eighth, four amendments), then credits the Third Amendment, which is in the second group.

## bill-of-rights-1
- 03-the-other-nine.mdx counts the Seventh (juries over money or property) among the amendments about crime suspects.

## bill-of-rights

- 02-incorporation.mdx Checkpoint says "claims like Cruikshank's"; Cruikshank was a perpetrator whose conviction was thrown out, not a rights claimant.
- 02-incorporation.mdx "ninety years of precedent" has no clear baseline.
- first-ten-amendments.mdx puts Engblom's "homes" in quotation marks; 03-the-quiet-amendments.mdx says "house".
- first-ten-amendments.mdx says "Congress shall make no law" "mattered enormously in Barron" without saying how.

- 01-what-it-held.mdx: Santa Clara (1886) is called "a century before this case"; it is 124 years.

## communitarianism
