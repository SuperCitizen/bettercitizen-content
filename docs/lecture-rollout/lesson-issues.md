# What the lecture writers found in the lessons

Writing a lecture script means checking it line by line against its lesson, so the writers noticed places where a lesson contradicts itself. No lesson was changed. Each line below is a place for an editor to look.

## score-the-vote-book
- what-is-score-voting.mdx: prose says Avila and Chen each have "two detractors who scored them at the floor", but the table gives Avila 0, 1, 0 and Chen 1, 0, 2. (Script says "at or near the floor".)
- what-is-score-voting.mdx: the figure says the highest city AVERAGE wins; the prose says highest TOTAL. (Script uses totals.)
- introduction.mdx: body says 64 percent for Fargo; its source note says 63.5.
- introduction.mdx: "Removed from above" sits beside a ban passed by Missouri VOTERS.
- introduction / the-plurality-problem / what-is-score-voting: frontmatter names a "Status-quo defender" perspective, but none of the three gives it an argument (and none has PerspectiveTabs).

## congress
- 01-two-chambers.mdx ends "Next in this pack: who actually wins… seats", but the pack has one lesson.
- 01-two-chambers.mdx frontmatter says Federalist 62 is "Hamilton or Madison"; the body says Madison.

## three-branches
- 02-checks-and-balances.mdx narrates the 1975 Train ruling, then says "Congress passed" the 1974 Act: reads out of order.

## three-branches-2
- 02-how-one-branch-stops-another.mdx: the body never says Bork and Garland were Supreme Court nominees (only its sources do).

## voter-id-and-mail-ballots
- 01-how-it-works.mdx: prose says 23 photo-ID states; its table sums to 24 (10 strict + 14 non-strict).
- 03-the-policy-landscape.mdx: 53 Republican seats, Murkowski the lone Republican no, yet 51 yes votes (the arithmetic does not close).
- 03-the-policy-landscape.mdx: the water-rule distance differs between the table and the checkpoint.

## how-bills-become-law-2
- 01-why-most-bills-die.mdx lists "One text" and "The President" under "The veto points Congress built", though its own text makes them constitutional.
- 01-why-most-bills-die.mdx gives no year for the Constitution; Hamilton's 1788 is only in the frontmatter. 02 never gives Hamilton's first name.

## bill-of-rights-2
- 01-who-it-protects-you-from.mdx: the figure caption says half the amendments concern investigating, trying and punishing (Fourth to Eighth); the prose puts the weight on "the third group" (Fifth to Eighth, four amendments), then credits the Third Amendment, which is in the second group.

## bill-of-rights-1
- 03-the-other-nine.mdx counts the Seventh (juries over money or property) among the amendments about crime suspects.

## bill-of-rights
- 02-incorporation.mdx intro says "forty-year refusal"; its own dates give 29 years (1868 to 1897) or 57 (to 1925).
- 02-incorporation.mdx Checkpoint says "claims like Cruikshank's"; Cruikshank was a perpetrator whose conviction was thrown out, not a rights claimant.
- 02-incorporation.mdx "ninety years of precedent" has no clear baseline.
- first-ten-amendments.mdx puts Engblom's "homes" in quotation marks; 03-the-quiet-amendments.mdx says "house".
- first-ten-amendments.mdx says "Congress shall make no law" "mattered enormously in Barron" without saying how.

## citizens-united
- 01-what-it-held.mdx, "What stood": "The same five Justices … joined the four dissenters" contradicts its own "8–1, with only Justice Thomas dissenting" and lesson 04's "four of the five in the majority".
- 01-what-it-held.mdx: Santa Clara (1886) is called "a century before this case"; it is 124 years.

## communitarianism
- Lessons 01 and 02 place Sandel in the civic-republican stream; lesson 03 says "Sandel, Reich, and Walzer are progressive communitarians".

## american-progressivism
- 01-what-it-believes.mdx says lesson 2 takes up the Third Way "betrayal or adaptation" argument "directly"; lesson 2 never names it.
- 03-cross-spectrum.mdx: "the civic-republican and traditionalist conservative streams" is ambiguous, because "civic-republican" is this pack's name for a progressive stream.

## american-conservatism
- 01-what-it-believes.mdx: the figure's four post-2016 branches and the body's four-bullet list do not match. The figure has traditionalists/post-liberals; the body has "Conservatism Inc." instead.

## federal-budget
- Lessons 2 and 3 price the 2025 reconciliation act differently: 4.7 trillion (CRFB, through 2035) and about 4.1 trillion (CBO, 2025–2034). Both are sourced, but a listener hears two totals.
- 01-where-the-money-goes.mdx: "about 1.8 trillion" minus 917 billion is given as "roughly 860 billion" (from 1,775); mental arithmetic on the rounded figure gives about 880.

## foreign-policy
- 03-the-policy-landscape.mdx says Lesson 1 "cataloged" the Ukraine responses; Lesson 1 covers only the Finland and Sweden accession.

## dark-money
- 03-the-policy-landscape.mdx says every floor defeat came "not from losing a majority", yet its 2022 vote was a 49–49 tie.
- 01-how-it-flows.mdx: 2020 is "more than $1 billion" in the table, "about $1 billion" in the text.

## climate
- 01-the-science-and-the-stakes.mdx: the figure cites a data file of October 9, 2026; the text says it was written in mid-2026.

## emergency-powers
- 03-reform-and-the-permanent-emergency.mdx, opening: "ten presidencies" since November 1979. Carter through Trump's second term is nine presidencies, eight presidents. (The script leaves the count out.)
- 01-the-constitution-of-emergency.mdx: the timeline is titled "Fourteen weeks", but its events run from April 1861 to March 1863.

## electoral-college
- 02-the-strongest-cases.mdx: "one certified total of 537" is a margin (lesson 03 says "certified for Bush by 537 votes"). (The script says "margin".)
- 02-the-strongest-cases.mdx: "flipping about 43,000 votes" in 2020 is the three states' combined margin (lesson 03: 42,918); the 1968 and 2004 counterfactuals count switched voters, which is half the margin.

## free-speech
- 01-what-the-first-amendment-protects.mdx lists three `perspectives:` in its frontmatter but never presents them.
- 03-platforms-and-the-modern-square.mdx says "early 1990s" but dates Prodigy 1995, and its hook has the app, not the law, sitting "unenforced".
- 03-platforms-and-the-modern-square.mdx never defines intermediate or strict scrutiny, standing, or remand.

## how-bills-become-law
- 03-why-bills-die.mdx lists the veto and override under "the chambers' own construction", straight after saying the Constitution built presentment.
- 02-how-laws-really-get-made.mdx cites "presentment is all-or-nothing" as a fact from Lesson 1; Lesson 1 never states it.
- 02-how-laws-really-get-made.mdx has `PerspectiveTabs` although the pack is `contested: false`.

## gerrymandering
- 02-the-strongest-cases.mdx, the realist's concession: "Lose elections to change the map" reads like a slip for "Win elections" (the core claim two paragraphs up says "win elections"). The script says "win elections".
- 02-the-strongest-cases.mdx: "Two months later" spans August to November 4.
- 02-the-strongest-cases.mdx puts "the Legislature of each State" in quotation marks, which is not the Constitution's wording.
- 03-where-the-courts-have-landed.mdx says commentators "named" the loop, but "Rucho two-step" only appears later.

## libertarianism
- 03-cross-spectrum.mdx: the scoreboard says only "Friedman", which is ambiguous after lesson 2's David Friedman.
- 02-internal-disagreements.mdx: the think-tank check names the "welfare and originalism rows" as the giveaway, though UBI has its own row.

## housing
- 02-the-strongest-cases.mdx: "the D.C.–Newark contrast" is ambiguous (both cities are described as having laws with teeth).
- 01-how-it-works.mdx, first Checkpoint: the question never says which metro is tightly zoned.

## lobbying
- 01-how-it-works.mdx says "each administration since Clinton's" imposed an ethics pledge; lesson 3 lists only Clinton, Obama, Trump and Biden.
- 02-the-strongest-cases.mdx, first Checkpoint: the answer is in effect the restriction-first reading given in the lesson's own voice, before the three cases are laid out. Worth an editor's look for balance. (The script keeps it as the rejoinder the question asks for and adds that the camps lean on different halves of it.)

## healthcare
- 01-how-it-works.mdx gives two per-person spending figures (15,474 and 14,775) and two GDP shares (18.0 and 17.2); the coverage-gap count is dated mid-2026 in the body and 2025 in a checkpoint.
- 02-the-strongest-cases.mdx: an objective promises market-reform concessions "about pre-ACA failures", and the lesson has none. "Taubman et al. 2014" is cited but is not in `sources:`.

## immigration
- 01-how-it-works.mdx says "Five statutes" but lists six.
- 01-how-it-works.mdx: the misconception box says a "plurality" overstayed visas; the body says about 42 percent.
- 03-the-policy-landscape.mdx: the hook compares December 2023 encounters with September 2025 apprehensions, and its Checkpoint says encounters are at 1970 levels.
- 03-the-policy-landscape.mdx is dated: the Barbara decision is "expected by early July 2026".
- 03-the-policy-landscape.mdx says state margins "moved in one direction", then describes divergence.
