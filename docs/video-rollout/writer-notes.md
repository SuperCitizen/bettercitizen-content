# Video rollout: what the writers flagged

Notes from the writers of the first video scripts (2026-11-23), kept as they reported them. Nothing here has been fixed or independently checked. "Lesson issues" are things that looked wrong in a lesson or assessment; "judgment calls" are places a writer was unsure a script was fully supported or balanced.

## Lesson issues

- congress: the lesson ends "Next in this pack: who actually wins House and Senate seats", but the pack has one lesson.
- constitution-basics / constitution-basics-1: amendment proposals are "more than 11,000" in one pack and "about 12,000" (figure: 11,985) in the other.
- dark-money 03: the lesson says FEC deadlocks rose from "about 3 percent"; assessment q4 says "about 2.9%".
- electoral-college 03: the prose says "three different mechanisms", but its table and MatchUp show four deciders.
- climate 02: titled "The strongest case for and against" though it gives three positions.
- foreign-policy assessment 03 q1: explanation says "roughly $165 billion subsequently ordered returned"; the lesson has only "as much as $175 billion".
- foreign-policy assessment 01 q2: the correct choice and explanation add detail (a purpose clause; Pakistan, Mali, Cameroon, Kenya) that is not in the lesson.
- foreign-policy assessment 01 q3: explanation names Major Non-NATO Allies the lesson does not.
- american-progressivism assessment 01 q4: the correct answer and "roughly 65%" are not in that lesson's body (the detail is in lesson 02).
- american-conservatism assessment 02 q3: an argument the lesson gives as the traditionalist critique is attributed to the classical-liberal one.
- housing assessment 03 q3: explanation says "roughly 1.2 million additional homes"; the lesson says "on the order of a million".
- immigration assessment 01 q3: explanation's "passed 12 million by 2007" is not in the lesson.
- socialism assessment 01 q1: the correct choice defines democratic socialism the way the lesson defines social democracy.
- socialism assessment 01 q3: Sanders's program details are not in the lesson.
- socialism 03: "the first eighteen months" of a mayoralty that began January 1, 2026, described "as of mid-2026".
- pacs-and-super-pacs assessment 02 q3: cites "BCRA" and "the 527 disclosure law of 2000"; the lesson names neither.
- pacs-and-super-pacs 03 and primaries-and-caucuses 03: the closing paragraph gives app instructions (`/me/positions/new`, a slug).
- the-administrative-state 02 and 03: written from "mid-June 2026", calling Trump v. Slaughter pending; probably stale.
- taxes 02: titled "The strongest case for and against" but gives three positions.
- free-speech, media-literacy: assessment files lack the numeric prefix their lessons have (harmless; the checker now matches by `lesson:`).

## Judgment calls

- congress video: "about 39.5 million" and "about 577,000" are the assessment's rounded forms; the lesson body gives exact census figures.
- constitution-basics 02: "must call" a convention where the lesson quotes "shall call".
- constitution-basics-1 03: Jefferson's coat line is paraphrased, not quoted.
- dark-money 02: stop q1 uses the lesson's Checkpoint scenario framing; the reformer's turn (about 50 words) is shorter than the centrist's (about 65).
- electoral-college 03: "five inversions" said flatly; the 1824 asterisk is only in the hand-off.
- citizens-united 02: runs about 3:29; the institutionalist turn is shorter, as in the lesson; each "what would change it" is compressed.
- citizens-united 04: Thomas's turn is shorter and has a point, not a key line.
- citizens-united 01: stop q2's correct answer names Taft-Hartley, which the video does not.
- citizens-united _intro: "corporations are people" framing comes from an assessment question, not a lesson body.
- bill-of-rights-2 01: the Brandenburg test is paraphrased.
- climate 02: the three positions share one scene, each with a point but no key line each.
- climate 03: stop q4 is taught only through the California example; RGGI is not named.
- city-councils _intro: key line paraphrases the lesson.
- communitarianism 02: stop q4 rests on a QuickCheck explanation.
- federalism-1 02: stop q3 has a treaties distractor the video never mentions.
- foreign-policy 03: the three schools get equal turns on only one front.
- foreign-policy _intro: "American forces have fought many times since" is a paraphrase of the lesson's list.
- Running time: about twenty lesson videos run a few seconds over three minutes while inside the word limits (among them citizens-united 02, american-conservatism 01 and 02, emergency-powers 01 and 03, federalism-2 01 and 02, foreign-policy 02, free-speech 01 and 03, immigration 02, lobbying 02, media-literacy 03, political-parties 01 and 02, three-branches-2 01 and 02).
- Contested lessons where the perspectives share one scene, each with a point but no key line of its own: climate 02, supreme-court 02 and 03, taxes 03, and the socialism and reading-the-numbers packs.
- Stops whose correct answer includes a detail the video does not teach: citizens-united 01 q2, federalism-1 02 q3, lobbying 01 q2, media-literacy 03 q4, pacs 02 q3, taxes 03 q3, three-branches-2 02 q4, climate 03 q4.
- federalism 03 and how-bills-become-law 02 and 03: not marked contested but the lessons give more than one view; each view got a turn.
- emergency-powers 03, federalism 02, the-administrative-state 02 and 03, healthcare 01, free-speech 03, how-bills-become-law-2 01: a quotation or phrase of the lesson is paraphrased, not quoted.
- school-boards 01 q1: "obligates districts to educate students with disabilities" is stronger wording than the lesson's.
- reading-the-numbers 02: the lesson favors the pragmatist position; the video gives it no preference.
- immigration 03: camp order follows the frontmatter, not the lesson's sentence.
- supreme-court 03: the expansion scene gives history only, not the arguments; supreme-court 01 cuts Kagan's quotation mid-sentence.
- three-branches-2 02: a point says "2021" after narration that says "In 2020"; the lesson has both.
- pacs 03: the restrictionist appears only unattributed, as in the lesson. political-parties 03: two of five reform families are argued, the rest named.
- Several intros use a fact from a lesson or an assessment, not from pack.yaml (allowed by the standard).
- voter-id-and-mail-ballots assessment 02 q2: choice (d) says "1,567 cases" while the question and the lesson say "more than 1,600". (A lesson issue, reported late.)
- voting-rights 02: Ginsburg's and Roberts's lines are close paraphrases, not marked quotations. voting-systems-overview 01: "By one February 2026 count" stands in for the lesson's attribution to FairVote.

## The two books

- Only four chapters of each book have an assessment file, so most chapter videos have no stops: 15 of 18 in ranked-choice-voting-book have none (stops only on what-is-ranked-choice-voting, alaska-the-first-state, the-data and constitutional-questions), and in score-the-vote-book chapters 11 to 19 only constitutional-and-legal-questions and the-objections have them. A chapter's inline QuickChecks cannot be used as stops.
- ranked-choice-voting-book: lessons say "this June" and "this November" (written mid-2026); the scripts use explicit dates or "as of mid-2026". Chapters 10 to 18 list three perspectives in frontmatter, but only why-this-matters-now has PerspectiveTabs.
- ranked-choice-voting-book, the-opposition: the three opposition arguments get a scene each; the author's answers get one shorter scene. why-this-matters-now: the status-quo turn is longer than the other two and omits its exhausted-ballots argument.
- score-the-vote-book, your-move: the lesson says "Twenty chapters of evidence" and "eighteen chapters"; the pack has 19. St. Louis approvals per ballot are "1.6" in the media chapter and "1.56" elsewhere.
- score-the-vote-book, the-objections: three of the five objections are argued, two only named; stop q4 uses 52/48 where the video's example is 51/49. the-strategy-question runs 423 words (a length warning).
- Both your-move videos report the author's advice to readers, which is close to asking something of the viewer.
- score-the-vote-book chapters 1 to 10: only what-is-score-voting and voter-satisfaction-efficiency have an assessment file, so the other eight have no stops.
- score-the-vote-book, the-reform-landscape: the lesson's heading says "five families of fixes" over a table of six rows; the video says "several".
- score-the-vote-book, where-score-voting-works: the narration keeps the author's "Both readings are honest, and I hold the first". the-strategy-question: "Colorado, Idaho, Oregon and Nevada all said no" compresses Nevada's refused second passage. voter-satisfaction-efficiency: the point "Plurality: 75%" is the figure's value; the lesson's table says roughly 70 to 85.
