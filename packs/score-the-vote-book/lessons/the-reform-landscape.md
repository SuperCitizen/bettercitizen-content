# The Reform Landscape

## We All Agree the System Is Broken

Before we make the case for score voting specifically, let's acknowledge the broader movement. Ranked choice voting, STAR voting, approval voting, score voting — these are all trying to solve the same fundamental problem: plurality voting fails to capture voter intent.

## Ranked Choice Voting (RCV)

RCV asks voters to rank candidates in order of preference. If no one gets a majority, the last-place candidate is eliminated and their voters' ballots transfer to their next choice. It's the most well-known reform, used in Alaska, Maine, New York City, and dozens of other jurisdictions.

RCV deserves credit for putting voting reform on the map. It's the reform most Americans have heard of, and it's proven that alternatives to plurality voting can work in real elections. That matters enormously.

## STAR Voting

STAR (Score Then Automatic Runoff) is a hybrid: voters score candidates 0-10, the two highest-scoring candidates advance to an automatic runoff, and the one preferred by more voters wins. It's clever — combining score voting's expressiveness with a majority-guarantee runoff step.

## Why This Book Focuses on Score Voting

All three systems are massive improvements over plurality. But they're not equal. When you compare them on the criteria that matter most — simplicity, resistance to strategic manipulation, voter satisfaction, and scalability — score voting comes out on top. The rest of this book explains why.
