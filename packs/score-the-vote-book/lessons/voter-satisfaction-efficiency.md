# Voter Satisfaction Efficiency

## The Gold Standard Metric

How do you objectively compare voting systems? You simulate them. Millions of times. With every imaginable distribution of voter preferences. Then you measure how often each system picks the candidate that maximizes overall voter satisfaction.

This metric is called Voter Satisfaction Efficiency (VSE), and it was developed by researchers to provide an apples-to-apples comparison.

## The Results

Across virtually all simulations:

- **Score voting** consistently scores highest in VSE, typically 95%+ even with strategic voters
- **STAR voting** comes close, typically 91-95%
- **Ranked choice voting (IRV)** scores lower, typically 79-90%
- **Plurality voting** is the worst, typically 70-80%

These aren't cherry-picked scenarios. These are comprehensive simulations across thousands of election configurations.

## What This Means in Plain English

Score voting picks the candidate that the most people are most satisfied with, more often than any other system. It finds the consensus winner — the candidate with the broadest, deepest support.

## RCV's Center-Squeeze Problem

RCV has a well-documented failure mode called "center squeeze": when a moderate candidate who would beat either extreme candidate head-to-head gets eliminated early because they have fewer passionate first-choice supporters. Score voting doesn't have this problem because every voter evaluates every candidate.
