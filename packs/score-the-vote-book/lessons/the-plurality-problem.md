# The Plurality Problem

## One Vote, Zero Nuance

Plurality voting — pick one, most votes wins — is the worst commonly used voting system in the world. That's not hyperbole. It's the conclusion of virtually every social choice theorist who's studied the question.

## The Spoiler Effect

When Ralph Nader ran in 2000, he didn't spoil the election because he was a bad candidate. He spoiled it because the system couldn't handle more than two options. Any voting system where voting honestly for your favorite can cause your least-favorite to win is fundamentally broken.

## Duverger's Law

Plurality voting mathematically trends toward two-party systems. This isn't a theory — it's an observable law of political science. The two-party stranglehold on American politics isn't a cultural accident. It's a mathematical inevitability of the ballot design.

## Strategic Voting Is the Norm

Under plurality, millions of Americans vote against candidates rather than for them. "Don't waste your vote" isn't cynicism — it's rational behavior in a broken system. The solution isn't to shame voters for being strategic. It's to give them a system where honesty is the best strategy.
