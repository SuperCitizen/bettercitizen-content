# The Objections

## "People Will Just Bullet Vote"

Some will. And when they do, score voting degrades gracefully into approval voting — which is still far better than plurality. Under RCV, strategic voting can cause perverse outcomes. Under score voting, the worst-case strategy still produces decent results.

## "It Doesn't Guarantee a Majority Winner"

Neither does plurality — the system we use now. And RCV's "majority" is often a majority of *remaining* ballots after exhaustion, not a true majority of voters. Score voting finds the candidate with the broadest support, which is more democratically meaningful than a manufactured majority.

## "It's Untested in Government Elections"

Approval voting (a simplified form of score voting) has been used in real U.S. elections. Score voting itself has been used in organizational and private elections. The theoretical and simulation evidence is overwhelming. Every voting reform was "untested" before someone tested it.

## "Voters Can't Handle It"

This is the same argument used against RCV, against women's suffrage, against direct election of senators. Voters handle far more complex decisions daily — mortgage comparisons, medical choices, consumer ratings. A 0-10 scale is not cognitively demanding.

## "What About Ties?"

Ties are vanishingly rare in score voting — more rare than in plurality, because the scoring scale provides much finer granularity. Standard tiebreaking procedures apply in the unlikely event.
