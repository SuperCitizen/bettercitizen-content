# Money and Elections

## The Runoff Cost Savings

Score voting eliminates the need for runoff elections entirely — there's always a decisive winner in a single round. Cities spend millions on runoffs with dismal turnout. Score voting saves that money and captures voter preferences in one shot.

## How Score Voting Changes Campaign Economics

When every voter can give you a score, the math of campaigning shifts. You don't just need your base — you need broad acceptability. This incentivizes:
- More positive campaigning (alienating any group risks zero scores)
- Broader coalition outreach (every voter's score counts)
- Less reliance on party machinery (you can win on personal merit)

## Third-Party Viability

Under plurality, third-party candidates are spoilers. Under score voting, they're just... candidates. You can give your third-party favorite a 10 and a major-party candidate an 8 without any strategic penalty. This levels the playing field for candidates without party war chests.
