# Simplicity Wins

## The Complexity Tax

Every voting system pays a complexity tax — the cognitive and administrative cost of being different from what people are used to. The lower that tax, the more likely a system is to be adopted, understood, and trusted.

## Score Voting: Rate Each Candidate

Score voting's instructions fit on a napkin: "Give each candidate a score from 0 to 10. Highest total wins." That's it. No rounds. No elimination. No transfers. No runoff. One ballot, one count, one winner.

## RCV's Complexity Problem

RCV is harder to explain. Rankings. Elimination rounds. Ballot transfers. Exhausted ballots. Most voters can fill out the ballot correctly, but fewer can explain *how the winner is determined*. When voters don't understand how their vote is counted, trust erodes — even if the system is working perfectly.

The 2021 NYC mayoral primary demonstrated this: 135,000 test ballots were accidentally included in preliminary results, and the multi-round tabulation made the error harder to catch and explain.

## STAR's Middle Ground

STAR is more complex than score voting (two stages instead of one) but simpler than RCV. It's a reasonable middle ground — but the automatic runoff step adds a layer that score voting simply doesn't need.

## Adoption Follows Simplicity

The simpler a reform is to explain, the easier it is to pass. Score voting has the lowest explanation barrier of any serious reform proposal.
