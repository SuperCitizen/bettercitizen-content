# STAR Voting: The Hybrid

## The Best of Both Worlds?

STAR voting (Score Then Automatic Runoff) is the most serious competitor to pure score voting. It uses score ballots (0-10) but adds a runoff step: the two highest-scoring candidates advance, and the one preferred on more ballots wins.

## What STAR Gets Right

STAR addresses the most common objection to score voting — that strategic bullet voting could dominate — by adding a preference-based runoff. It guarantees that the winner is preferred by a majority in the final round. It's expressive, relatively simple, and performs well in simulations.

## Where STAR Adds Unnecessary Complexity

The runoff step, while clever, adds complexity without proportional benefit:

- It's one more step to explain to voters
- It can produce winners who scored *lower* overall than the runner-up (the higher-scoring candidate can lose the runoff)
- The guarantee of a "majority winner" in the runoff is somewhat illusory — it's a majority between two pre-selected candidates, not a true majority mandate

## The Simplicity Argument Again

Score voting's greatest strength is its transparency: highest score wins. Period. STAR muddies this with a second stage that can override the score results. For voters who want to understand *why* their candidate won or lost, score voting is clearer.

## STAR as Stepping Stone

If your jurisdiction is considering STAR voting, support it — it's vastly better than plurality. But if you're choosing between STAR and score voting for a new reform campaign, the simpler option with equal or better outcomes is the stronger choice.
