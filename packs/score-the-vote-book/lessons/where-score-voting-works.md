# Where Score Voting Works

## It's Already Everywhere (Just Not in Politics)

Score voting isn't some untested theory. It's the most widely used decision-making system in the world — just not for government elections. Yet.

- **Olympics**: Judges score athletes on a scale. Highest total wins.
- **Amazon, Yelp, Google**: Star ratings are score voting for products and services.
- **Hot or Not, Rate My Professor, IMDb**: Score-based ranking is how the internet makes decisions.
- **Hiring committees**: Score candidates on criteria, pick the highest scorer.
- **Wine competitions, film festivals, talent shows**: Judges score, highest wins.

Humans intuitively understand scoring. We do it constantly. The only place we *don't* use it is the one place it matters most.

## Political Implementations

Score voting has been used in organizational elections, party primaries, and small-scale political elections. The evidence from these implementations confirms the theoretical predictions: more expressive ballots, higher satisfaction, simpler tabulation.

## Approval Voting: Score Voting's Cousin

Approval voting (score each candidate 0 or 1) has been adopted in Fargo, North Dakota and St. Louis, Missouri for municipal elections. It's a simplified version of score voting, and its success in real elections demonstrates voter comfort with the "evaluate every candidate" paradigm.
