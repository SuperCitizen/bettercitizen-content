# RCV: Credit Where It's Due

## The Trailblazer

This book argues that score voting is the better system. But intellectual honesty demands we acknowledge what ranked choice voting has accomplished — because without RCV, the entire voting reform conversation might not exist.

## RCV Put Reform on the Map

Maine. Alaska. New York City. Minneapolis. San Francisco. Dozens of cities and two states. RCV has proven that Americans will vote for voting reform when given the chance. It has normalized the idea that plurality voting is broken and fixable.

## Real Improvements

RCV has produced measurable improvements over plurality:
- Less negative campaigning (candidates need second-choice votes)
- More diverse candidates running and winning
- Elimination of costly runoff elections
- Higher voter engagement in some jurisdictions

## Where RCV Falls Short

But RCV also has real limitations that its advocates often downplay:
- Center-squeeze failures (Burlington 2009)
- Non-monotonicity (giving a candidate *more* support can cause them to *lose*)
- Ballot exhaustion (your vote can effectively disappear in later rounds)
- Complex tabulation that's harder to audit
- Higher rates of ballot errors due to ranking complexity

These aren't theoretical complaints — they're documented outcomes from real RCV elections.

## The Ally, Not the Answer

RCV is an ally in the reform movement. It's proven the concept. But the movement shouldn't stop at RCV just because it got there first. Score voting builds on what RCV proved while avoiding its pitfalls.
