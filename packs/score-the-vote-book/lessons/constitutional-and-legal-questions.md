# Constitutional and Legal Questions

## Is Score Voting Legal?

Yes. The Constitution gives states broad authority over election methods. Nothing requires plurality voting. Courts have upheld RCV and approval voting — score voting sits on the same legal foundation.

## "One Person, One Vote"

The most common legal objection: "Doesn't scoring multiple candidates violate one person, one vote?" No. Each voter gets one ballot with equal weight. The principle of "one person, one vote" requires equal weighting — not a single mark. Just as rating every Olympic skater doesn't give any judge extra votes, scoring every candidate doesn't give any voter extra power.

## Existing Precedent

Approval voting (a form of score voting with only two score levels) has survived legal challenges in Fargo and St. Louis. This precedent supports the legality of expanded-scale score voting.

## Implementation Requirements

Score voting is actually easier to implement than RCV from a technical standpoint. Standard optical-scan ballots can handle score voting with minimal modification. Tabulation is simple addition — no special software required.
