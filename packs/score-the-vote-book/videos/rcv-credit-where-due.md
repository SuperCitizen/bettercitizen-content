## The vote to save the vote

In October 2017, less than a year after Maine's voters passed ranked-choice voting, the legislature passed a law shelving it. In June 2018, voters overrode their own legislature. That same day they used ranked ballots to pick their party nominees: the first statewide ranked-choice election in American history.

- June 2018: Maine's people's veto, 53.88%

This is a chapter of Score the Vote. It is an argument, and the "I" in it is the book's author. I am writing a book about why score voting is better than ranked-choice voting. So let's start with the truth.

> Ranked-choice voting put electoral reform on the American map.

## What it has actually won

Alaska adopted its system in 2020 by 3,781 votes, and kept it in 2024 by 743. New York City adopted ranked-choice voting in 2019, with 73.5 percent. By FairVote's February 2026 count, 49 American jurisdictions use it.

- New York City: 73.5%
- Alaska: kept by 743 votes
- 49 jurisdictions

One instant runoff replaces a separate runoff election, which is expensive and notoriously low-turnout. And nineteen state legislatures have now banned it. Nobody bans an idea that isn't winning.

> American voters will change the ballot itself, and defend the change.

## The one I can't wave away

Alaska, August 2022, a special election for the U.S. House. Nick Begich was eliminated first, with 28.53 percent of first choices. Mary Peltola then beat Sarah Palin, 51.48 to 48.52.

Here is the problem. Two mathematicians, Adam Graham-Squire and David McCune, analyzed the full ballot data. Begich would have beaten Peltola head-to-head, and Palin head-to-head.

- Begich: out first, 28.53%
- Begich beat each rival head-to-head

For honesty's sake: across 182 American instant-runoff elections, they found exactly two that failed this way. Rare, then. But it strikes in polarized three-way races, the races reform exists to fix.

- 2 failures in 182 elections

> The center squeeze: the instant runoff eliminated the consensus candidate first.

## Ally, not answer

The people who call Alaska 2022 a rigged election are wrong on the arithmetic. Peltola won the final head-to-head count. Ranked-choice voting is better than what it replaced.

- Better than what it replaced

But better than plurality is a low bar. The documented weaknesses, I argue, share a common root.

> Ranked ballots collect rich information. Elimination-style counting wastes it.

Not settled: whether any alternative avoids these failures without buying worse ones. Every voting system makes trade-offs.

- Not settled: do the alternatives do better?
