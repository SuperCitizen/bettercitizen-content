## The 13 million dollar runoff

In October 2013, New York City held a runoff for public advocate, an office with a yearly budget of 2.3 million dollars. About seven percent of voters turned out. The runoff cost roughly 13 million dollars.

- Runoff: roughly $13 million
- Office budget: $2.3 million
- Turnout: about 7%

This is a chapter of Score the Vote. It is an argument for score voting, and the "I" in it is the book's author. That runoff is the one money claim voting reformers can make with receipts. The rest is murkier.

## The donor's veto

Under pick-one rules, a candidate's first task is not persuading voters. It is persuading the people who fund campaigns that she can win. Support for a candidate who cannot win helps the candidate her supporters like least, and donors know it. So every outsider meets the same sentence: do not waste your money on a spoiler.

- Viability comes before voters

Nobody has to be corrupt for this to work. Everyone only has to count.

> The duopoly isn't protected by law. It's protected by arithmetic.

## What the evidence shows, and does not

Verified: when a single ballot replaces a separate second election, the runoff's cost disappears. That saving comes from consolidating rounds, not from score ballots as such.

- Verified: runoff savings

Not established: that any alternative ballot reduces total campaign spending, super PAC activity or donor influence. Maine and Alaska did not become low-money states.

- Not established: less money in politics

> That ballot reform shrinks money in politics: not supported by current evidence.

Anyone who tells you a ballot reform will get money out of politics is selling past the evidence. When my side does it, you should wince.

## The entry price

Under score rules, supporting an outsider is free. You can give the independent a 10 and a major-party candidate an 8, and you have surrendered nothing. Without the wasted-vote argument, the donor's veto loses its enforcement.

- Outsider 10, major party 8

That is a structural prediction. No state has run a score election, so nothing has tested it.
