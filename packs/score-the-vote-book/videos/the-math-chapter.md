## What Arrow said

In 2012, a podcast host asked Kenneth Arrow which voting system he would recommend. Arrow wrote the most famous impossibility result in social science. His answer was hedged: "I'm a little inclined to think that score systems where you categorize in maybe three or four classes... is probably the best."

- Kenneth Arrow, 2012

This is a chapter of Score the Vote. It is an argument for score voting, and the "I" in it is the book's author.

## Two theorems

In 1951, Arrow proved that no method for turning individual rankings into a group ranking can meet a short list of fairness conditions all at once. The theorem's inputs are rankings. A score ballot is a different input, so score voting sits outside it.

- Arrow, 1951: rankings

My side oversells that. In 1973, Allan Gibbard proved that every voting scheme without chance or a dictator, and with more than two possible outcomes, can be manipulated. Rankings, scores or approvals: the proof does not care.

- Gibbard, 1973: every ballot type

> No voting system is strategy-proof. Ask what strategy does to each one.

## The election I built against myself

One hundred voters, two candidates. 55 voters score A a 5 and B a 4. The other 45 score A a 0 and B a 5.

[beat]

A totals 275. B totals 445. B wins in a landslide, while 55 percent of voters prefer A.

- A: 275 points
- B: 445 points

> Score voting fails the majority criterion. An honest advocate must say so.

Here is why I accept it. The majority is almost equally happy either way. For the minority, the difference is everything. If you hold instead that the majority must prevail however mild its preference, you are making a value choice, and score voting is honestly not your system.

## A forced trade

Ranked choice, counted by instant runoff, passes a test called later-no-harm: a backup choice can never hurt your favorite. Score voting fails it. Score voting passes the mirror test, favorite betrayal: honesty about your favorite never backfires. Instant runoff fails that one.

- Later-no-harm: instant runoff passes
- Favorite betrayal: score voting passes

> Every method buys its virtues with named, provable failures.

The lesson has the full scorecard, a three-way election counted by three rules, and what happens when that majority exaggerates. Which trade to value, the math cannot settle.
