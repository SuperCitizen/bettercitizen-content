## One mark

You walk into a booth. You pick one name. You leave. That single mark is supposed to carry your entire political judgment.

This is Score the Vote, a book by Chris Borgia. It is an argument, made in its author's first person, for score voting: give every candidate a score, and the highest total wins.

> Rating beats ranking. That is the case this book argues.

## Nineteen chapters

The first three say what is wrong with the pick-one ballot and how a score ballot works.

- 1 to 3: the problem and the method

The next eight weigh the rival reforms, ranked-choice and STAR among them, on simulations, real elections and the math.

- 4 to 11: the rivals, weighed

Five more take on primaries, the media, money, the law, and the best objections. The author concedes two of them.

- 12 to 16: the hard questions

The last three are a playbook built on Fargo and St. Louis, defeats included.

- 17 to 19: the playbook

## Where to start

The author's rule is that when the strongest argument is against him, you will hear it from him first. Each lesson also names the other perspectives on its question.

> An argument, not a neutral survey.

Start with the Introduction, The Ballot Is Broken. It opens with three ballots and one voter.
