## You already know how

You did this the last time you rated a restaurant, or argued about whether a movie deserved four stars or five. Score voting puts that on a ballot. Give every candidate a score on a fixed scale, say 0 to 5. The highest total wins.

- No rounds, no eliminations, no transfers

This is a chapter of Score the Vote. It is an argument, and the "I" in it is the book's author.

> Score every candidate. Highest total wins. That's the whole system.

## A count you can check

Five voters score four candidates: Avila, Brooks, Chen and Dimas. Tallying is addition. Avila totals 11, Chen 13, Dimas 8, and Brooks wins with 18.

- Brooks: 18
- Chen: 13
- Avila: 11
- Dimas: 8

Now the shape of that win. Not one voter scored Brooks a 5. Avila and Chen each have two passionate supporters, and two detractors who scored them at the floor. Brooks is the candidate every single voter scored a 3 or a 4.

> Brooks is nobody's favorite, and the one the whole town can live with.

Run the same five voters through a pick-one election. Avila and Chen tie at two votes each, and the count never sees Brooks.

## Every row is its own question

Here is the property that does the most work. Every candidate's score is independent. Your 5 for your favorite takes nothing from your 4 for your fallback, so you can support both, at the levels you actually feel.

- Each score is independent

> Support your favorite and your fallback. There is nothing to waste.

## The scale matters less than you'd think

Approval voting is score voting on the coarsest possible scale: approve, or don't. In Jameson Quinn's voter-satisfaction simulations, that two-level ballot already reaches roughly 89 to 95 percent of ideal voter satisfaction. Widening to eleven levels buys about 96 percent, with honest voters.

- Two levels: roughly 89 to 95%
- Eleven levels: about 96%

The structure does almost all the work: judge everyone, independently. What I'd insist on is the zero, a mark that means opposition.

## Two warnings

You should hear the catches from me first. A tactical voter's best play is usually to push scores to the endpoints, 5s and 0s. That compresses score voting toward approval voting.

- Tactics compress scores toward approval

And score voting elects the candidate with the broadest, deepest support. That is not always the candidate a bare majority would pick in a head-to-head fight.

- Broadest support, not always the majority's pick

Whether that is the right target for an election is not settled here. The lesson has the ballot itself, the count voter by voter, and the evidence on scales.
