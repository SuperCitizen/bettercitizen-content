## A map war between censuses

In August 2025, the Texas legislature redrew its congressional map four years early, in open pursuit of five more Republican seats. Two months later, California's voters answered. Proposition 50 passed with 64 percent, shelving the state's independent commission map for one drawn by the legislature, worth roughly five Democratic seats. The override is temporary: the commission resumes after the 2030 census.

- Texas: 5 more Republican seats sought
- California: roughly 5 Democratic seats
- Voter-approved, until the 2030 census

Three positions have to explain that season. Each one thinks it proves their point.

## The reform advocate

The reform advocate argues that partisan gerrymandering severs the connection between votes and seats, so the election, the voters' one corrective, is rigged against correction. They point to commission-drawn maps, which showed less partisan bias in the 2021 round. The entry that stings is California, where reformers themselves suspended a model commission to fight a national map war.

- Commission maps: less partisan bias

> The reform case: no state can safely stop alone.

## The partisan realist

The partisan realist argues that redistricting has always been political, and that a commission relocates the politics without removing it. They point to 1788, when Patrick Henry's allies in Virginia drew James Madison's congressional district to force him to face James Monroe. The gerrymander is older than the Bill of Rights. The entry that stings is Indiana, where the Republican senate refused a redraw, 31 to 19.

- 1788: Madison's district, drawn against him

> The realist case: maps follow power.

[check q1]

## The federalist

The federalist argues that the Constitution assigns congressional elections to each state's legislature, with a power in Congress to alter the rules, and no role for federal courts. They point to Utah, where state courts enforced the voters' Proposition 4 against their own legislature, without a single federal judge. The entry that stings is Texas, whose map was answered not by Texans but by Californians.

- Utah: state courts, no federal judge

> The federalist case: states and Congress decide, not federal courts.

## Settled and contested

Some of this is settled. Partisan gerrymandering exists, it produces measurable distortions, and both parties now redraw mid-decade. What elections are for, and whether neutral rules are a coherent category at all, is contested.

- Settled: it exists and is measurable
- Contested: whether neutral rules exist
