## A party splits in Reno

In May 2022, in a Reno convention hall, the Libertarian Party tore itself in half in public. An insurgent caucus swept the leadership elections, and several state affiliates and longtime donors walked away.

It looked like a personality fight in a small party. It was the oldest argument in libertarianism, surfacing.

> If coercion is presumptively wrong, how much state survives?

## The minarchist stream

Minarchists hold that the state is legitimate at minimum scope: protecting rights against force and fraud, enforcing contracts, providing courts and national defense. Robert Nozick argues that such a state would emerge from a stateless market for protection, as clients cluster toward a dominant agency, without anyone designing it and without violating rights along the way.

- Nozick, 1974: the night-watchman state

> The minarchist case: a minimal state is compatible with libertarian principle.

## The anarcho-capitalist stream

Anarcho-capitalists hold that the state is illegitimate at any scale. Murray Rothbard argues that courts, police and defense can all be supplied by providers competing for willing customers. If taxation without consent is wrong for one service, the argument runs, it is wrong for all of them.

- Rothbard, 1973: no state, competing law

> The anarcho-capitalist case: taxation without consent is wrong for every service.

## The bleeding-heart stream

Bleeding-heart libertarians hold that libertarian institutions are justified partly by what they do for the least advantaged, and that they need moral preconditions: a basic floor and genuine equality of opportunity. John Tomasi argues that market economies have the strongest record of raising the floor.

- Tomasi, 2012: markets plus social justice

> The bleeding-heart case: free institutions need a basic floor for the least advantaged.

## Where the family splits

On drugs, marriage and war, the family is nearly unanimous. That is why outsiders often cannot see the fault lines at all.

- Nearly unanimous: drugs, marriage, war

Welfare is one of the rows that carry the heat. Minarchists say minimal, with private charity first. Anarcho-capitalists say abolish. Bleeding-heart libertarians say replace it with a universal basic income.

- Welfare: minimal, abolish, or basic income

## Not settled

The lesson does not settle whether minarchy is a resting point or a way station, whether private law can scale, or whether social justice completes the tradition or dissolves it.

- Three open questions

It gives each stream's case against the other two, in its own voice, and a table of where each lands, issue by issue.
