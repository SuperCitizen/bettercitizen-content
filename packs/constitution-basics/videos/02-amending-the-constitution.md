## Thirty-eight states, and no amendment

Thirty-eight states, exactly the number Article Five requires, have voted at some point to ratify the Equal Rights Amendment. It is not in the Constitution.

[beat]

Meanwhile, an amendment that sat ignored for two centuries is in the Constitution, certified in 1992 without anyone seriously disputing it.

> Same article, same machinery, opposite outcomes.

## The machine

Proposal comes first. It takes two-thirds of both chambers of Congress, or a national convention that Congress must call when 34 state legislatures apply for one. That second track has never been used.

- Propose: 2/3 of both chambers
- Convention track: never used

Ratification follows. It takes three-fourths of the states, 38 of 50. The President has no role at any stage.

- Ratify: 38 of 50 states

## The one locked clause

Article Five ends with one permanent exception. No state, without its consent, can be deprived of its equal vote in the Senate. An amendment giving California more senators than Wyoming would need the consent of every state losing ground.

> Equal votes in the Senate: the one rule the machine cannot reach.

[check q4-entrenchment]

## Where the deadline lives

Nothing in Article Five mentions time limits. A proposal with no deadline stays pending, which is how a pay amendment from 1789 could be ratified in 1992.

- No deadline: still pending

Congress first attached a deadline in 1917, and where it puts one matters enormously. The D.C. voting rights amendment carried its seven-year limit inside the amendment's own text. When the clock ran out in 1985, there was nothing left to argue about. The Equal Rights Amendment's seven-year limit sat in the proposing resolution, outside the text the states ratified. Whether that kind of deadline binds is unresolved.

- In the amendment's text: it binds
- In the proposing resolution: contested

> A deadline inside the text binds. One outside it is contested.

[check q2-deadline-placement]

## Undecided

The Equal Rights Amendment's count stalled at 35 states, and the deadline passed in 1982. Three more states ratified between 2017 and 2020. Four states had voted to rescind. On one accounting it is ratified by 38 states. On another it has been expired since 1982. On neither has it been certified.

- 38 states on one accounting
- Expired since 1982 on another

> As of mid-2026, whether the amendment is validly ratified is undecided.

The lesson walks the full timeline and the court cases behind it, the convention nobody has called, and the evidence that this is among the hardest constitutions in the world to amend.
