## Shared before it was checked

After Hurricane Helene, a picture of a sobbing girl in a rescue boat tore across social media. She did not exist. Some who shared the picture kept it up anyway.

> The skills that work are few, fast and teachable.

## Three lessons

The first lesson shows how the information ecosystem works: who pays for the news, what a feed ranks for, and what happened to local papers.

- 1. How the information ecosystem works

The second is the verification toolkit: the moves professional fact-checkers use, and the research on which of them work.

- 2. The verification toolkit

The third takes up a contested question: who should decide what is true? Professional gatekeepers, decentralized crowds and a literate citizenry each get their strongest case, and none is declared the winner.

- 3. Who decides what's true?

Every lesson is honest about limits. Trusting nothing is the one outcome this pack refuses to teach.

## Where to start

Start with the first lesson, How the information ecosystem works. It begins in 1835, with a newspaper that discovered life on the moon.

- Start with lesson 1
