## The girl in the boat

In October 2024, after Hurricane Helene, an image tore across social media. A little girl in a life vest, sobbing in a rescue boat, clutching a puppy.

The girl did not exist. The image was AI-generated. Some people who shared it, told exactly that, declined to take it down.

- Hurricane Helene, October 2024

> The fake only needed to be shared faster than anyone checked.

## What the fact-checkers did

Stanford researchers put historians, students and professional fact-checkers in front of unfamiliar websites. The historians and students stayed on the page. They judged it by its design, its footnotes, its About page. Many were taken in.

- Staying on the page: often fooled

The fact-checkers left the page within seconds. They opened new tabs, and checked what the rest of the web said about the source. They were more accurate, in a fraction of the time. This is called lateral reading.

- Lateral reading: leave the page

> A website's surface is testimony from the defendant. Lateral reading cross-examines.

## Four moves

The practice has been compressed into four moves, and together they often take under a minute. Stop, the moment content gives you a surge of outrage or fear. Investigate the source. Find better coverage of the claim. And trace claims, quotes and media to the original.

- Stop
- Investigate the source
- Find better coverage
- Trace to the original

That last move matters, because most viral misinformation is not made from nothing. It is real material cut from its context, like a quote ended at the comma that reversed it. Get to the original, and judge that.

[check q2]

## When searching makes it worse

There is a catch. In one set of experiments, people who searched online to judge false stories ended up believing them more. A fresh false claim often exists online almost only in the words of the people pushing it. Search its exact phrasing, and you get the lie, mirrored. This is called a data void.

- Data void: the lie, mirrored

So search the event in neutral terms, and weigh the sources you have checked, not whatever ranked first.

- Search the event, in neutral terms

## Not "trust nothing"

The lesson ends on a warning: trusting nothing is not the goal. The goal is calibrated trust, which means confidence in proportion to a source's track record.

> A citizen who believes nothing is the propagandist's product.
