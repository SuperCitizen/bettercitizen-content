## Life on the moon

In August 1835, the New York Sun began a six-part exclusive. A famous astronomer, it said, had discovered life on the moon: unicorns, and winged bat-people who built temples.

None of it was true. Sales soared.

- The New York Sun, 1835

The Sun cost a penny. Its plan was new: sell the paper cheap, build a mass audience, and sell that audience to advertisers.

> The hoax was not a glitch in the model. It was the model, working.

## Who pays for this?

The most useful question in media literacy is not, is this true? It is, who pays for this, and what does that make them want from me?

- Who pays, and what do they want?

Take a free news app with no subscription. It is selling your attention to advertisers. So what it wants from you is time spent and engagement, not accuracy or importance.

- It sells your attention to advertisers

That does not prove any given story false. It tells you which errors to expect: toward the arresting, the emotional, and more of what you already liked.

> Funding tells you which errors to expect, not which stories are false.

## Two hundred logos

Most news you meet was not reported by the account showing it to you. Suppose the same earthquake story appears, nearly word for word, on 200 small news sites within an hour.

[beat]

That is almost certainly one wire-service story, republished 200 times. It is one act of reporting, not 200 confirmations. Everything after it is repackaging and commentary.

- One act of reporting, many copies

> Two hundred logos can equal exactly one source.

## Leakier than the slogan

A popular story says the feed seals each of us in a separate reality. The largest test to date complicates it. Researchers switched tens of thousands of users to a feed in time order for three months. They saw less time on the platform, and no significant change in polarization, political knowledge or attitudes.

- Three months: no significant change in polarization

Engagement ranking does shape what circulates. The walls are real, and leakier than the slogan.
