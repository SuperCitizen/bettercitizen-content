## Four in the morning, 1877

A few minutes past four in the morning on March 2, 1877, the president of the Senate announced that Rutherford B. Hayes had been elected President, 185 electoral votes to 184. Inauguration was two days away. The man who had won the popular vote by three points was not Hayes.

- Hayes elected, 185 to 184

Sixty presidential elections have been run under the Electoral College. Five went to the candidate who did not lead the popular vote.

> Five inversions in sixty elections, and not one failure repeated five times.

## 1876: the invented commission

Samuel Tilden, who led the popular vote, stood at 184 undisputed electoral votes, one short of a majority. Twenty more, from four states, were disputed, and Congress had been sent competing certificates. Which certificates were the real ones? On that question the Constitution was silent.

[beat]

So Congress invented a commission of fifteen. It awarded every disputed vote to Hayes, 8 to 7, on party lines.

- Competing certificates from four states
- A 15-member commission, 8 to 7

## 1888: the clean case

In 1888 there was no commission, no House vote and no disputed slate. Grover Cleveland won the popular vote by roughly 89,000 and lost the presidency, 233 electoral votes to 168. Benjamin Harrison's votes were simply distributed better. He won New York by 1.09 percent, and took all 36 of its electors.

- Cleveland: more votes, 168 electors
- Harrison: New York by 1.09%

> 1888: winner-take-all arithmetic, and nothing else.

## How each one was decided

Lay the five side by side. In 1824 the House chose, one vote per state. In 1876 it was the commission. In 2000 it was one state's certified margin of 537 votes, after the Supreme Court halted the recount. Only 1888 and 2016 were winner-take-all arithmetic alone.

- 1824: the House
- 1876: the commission
- 2000: Florida, by 537 votes
- 1888 and 2016: arithmetic

## Indictment or track record

What the record means is contested, and each camp has its reading. The defender points to five anomalies in sixty elections, every one absorbed. The reformer points to a failure rate that is concentrating, with two inversions in the seven elections since 2000. The preservationist points to the statutes of 1887 and 2022, which hardened the count.

- Defender: five in sixty, all absorbed
- Reformer: two since 2000
- Preservationist: statutes hardened the count
