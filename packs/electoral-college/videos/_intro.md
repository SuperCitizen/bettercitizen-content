## Five times

Five elections have ended with the popular-vote loser winning the presidency. The institution that produced those results was a contested compromise from the day it was drafted, and it is a live political question now.

> Five elections went to the candidate with fewer votes.

## Four lessons

The first lesson shows how the Electoral College actually works: 538 electors, 270 to win, and what happens if nobody gets there.

- 1. How it works

The second gives the strongest case for each of three positions: the originalist defender, the democratic-majority reformer, and the federalist preservationist. None is declared the winner.

- 2. The strongest case for and against

The third goes through the record, from the tie of 1800 to the count of 2024.

- 3. How it has played out

The fourth covers the reform debate: a compact among the states, district allocation, and abolition by amendment.

- 4. The reform debate
