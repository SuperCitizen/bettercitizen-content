## Nineteen laws

This is a chapter of Rank the Vote. It is an argument, and the "I" in it is the book's author.

On March 17, 2026, the governor of Ohio signed a law that forbids Ohio's cities from trying ranked choice voting. Any city that does forfeits its state local-government funding. Ohio was the nineteenth state to enact a ban.

- Ohio: the 19th state ban

My reading: nobody passes nineteen laws against a thing that is failing on its own.

## The measurements

In 2022, Pew found that 72 percent of Republicans called Democrats more immoral than other Americans, and 63 percent of Democrats said the same of Republicans. And in 2024, by Unite America's analysis, 87 percent of U.S. House races were effectively decided in the primary, in elections where about 7 percent of Americans voted.

- "Immoral": 72% and 63%
- 87% of House races
- About 7% of Americans voted

> The measurements are settled. The inference is contested.

## The inference

I believe, and I label this as argument, not data, that a window for reform is open now, and that the ban wave is what its closing looks like. Adoption is slow by design. A ban needs one legislative session.

> My argument, not data: a reform window is open, and closing.

## Three positions

The lesson gives three positions.

The reform advocate argues that the measurements are the case. Ranked choice voting is the most tested, most incremental fix available, with two states and dozens of cities as working proof. Move now.

- Reform advocate: move now

The status-quo defender argues that five statewide electorates heard a year of argument and said no. Plurality voting is simple, fast and familiar, and legislatures that set election rules are doing their constitutional job. The reform can win by persuasion, without urgency.

- Status-quo defender: persuade, without urgency

The alternative-reform advocate argues that single-winner ranked voting delivers modest gains at best, and that the deeper disease is single-winner districts. A rare window may be better spent on proportional representation or fusion voting.

- Alternative-reform advocate: a different reform

The lesson gives each of the three its full case, with the swing-seat count, the timeline of the bans, and the earlier floods of American reform.
