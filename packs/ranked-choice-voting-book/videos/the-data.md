## Two numbers, one election

In June 2021, New York City ran a ranked-choice election for mayor. Ninety-five percent of voters told exit pollsters the ballot was simple to complete. In that same election, roughly 15 percent of ballots ranked neither of the two finalists.

- 95% found the ballot simple
- About 15% ranked neither finalist

Advocates quote the first number. Opponents quote the second. Both are real.

This is a chapter of Rank the Vote. It is an argument, and the "I" in it is the book's author. Here is the ledger, including what cuts against me.

## Turnout

You have probably heard that ranked choice voting increases turnout. The careful version is less exciting. David Kimball and Joseph Anthony compared ranked cities to similar plurality cities. They found no statistically significant change in general-election turnout.

- Direct effect on turnout: modest, mixed

The strong evidence is structural: where ranking replaces a separate runoff election. FairVote examined 276 federal primary runoffs, from 1994 to 2022. Turnout fell between rounds in 96 percent of them, with a median decline of 40 percent.

- Runoffs: median turnout drop of 40%

> The turnout case is strongest where ranking replaces a separate runoff.

## Civility

In 2023, Todd Donovan and Caroline Tolbert asked whether the evidence fits the civility story. Their answer: only partly. Voters do perceive less negativity. About a third report candidates asking for second or third rankings. But the effect did not extend to frontrunners.

- Voters perceive less negativity
- No effect on frontrunners

> The incentive is a thumb on the scale, not a personality transplant.

## The costs

Burnett and Kogan analyzed more than 600,000 ballot images from four elections. Between 9.6 and 27.1 percent of first-round ballots exhausted before the final round. In all four, the winner finished with less than half of total votes cast: 43.4 to 45.7 percent.

- Exhausted: 9.6% to 27.1%
- Winners: 43.4% to 45.7% of all votes

That is the factual core of the "faux majority" charge, and I do not dispute the arithmetic. I dispute the baseline. All four elections capped voters at three rankings.
