## Seven hundred forty-three votes

This is the last chapter of Rank the Vote. It is an argument, and the "I" in it is the book's author.

Seven hundred forty-three votes. That was the entire margin, after a recount, by which Alaskans kept their election system in November 2024: 160,973 to 160,230.

- 743 votes, after a recount

> Margins like Alaska's are made, vote by vote.

## Know your terrain

The chapter starts with three questions about where a reader stands. Does your state have the initiative? Twenty-six states offer a statewide initiative or referendum process. Has your state banned ranked choice voting? As of mid-2026, nineteen have, and a ban is a statute, which the same majorities can repeal. Can your city move alone? Much of the movement's history is city charters, not state law.

- Initiative: 26 states
- Bans: 19 states, as of mid-2026
- Cities: charters, not state law

## The case the way it wins

Lead with outcomes, not machinery: majority winners, no more spoilers, a runoff without a second election. The mechanism comes second, after someone asks how.

- Outcomes first, mechanics second

Then concede what must be conceded. The center squeeze is real. The money evidence is thin. Civility gains are bounded. Statewide voters said no five times in 2024.

- Concede: four things

> Overclaiming is how advocates manufacture their own backlash.

## What is still being decided

None of this is finished. Alaska votes on repeal for a third time on November 3, 2026. Washington, D.C., is running its first ranked elections. Nineteen statehouses hold bans.

- Alaska: repeal vote, round three
