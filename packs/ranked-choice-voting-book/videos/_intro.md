## The question the ballot never asked

In November 2000, the presidency came down to 537 votes in Florida. On those same ballots, 97,488 people voted for Ralph Nader. Whom would they have chosen second? Nobody knows, and nobody can know.

> The ballot never asked.

## A book that argues a case

Rank the Vote is a book by Chris Borgia, and it is an argument. It makes the case for ranked choice voting in its author's first person, and it is marked contested.

- An argument, in the author's voice

Its eighteen chapters move from problem to proof to playbook. First, what the pick-one ballot discards, and how a ranked count works. Then the evidence from Alaska, Maine and two decades of American cities.

- The problem, the mechanics, the evidence

Then the opposition's strongest arguments get a full chapter, followed by the research, costs and all, and a century abroad.

- The opposition, the data, the world

The second half covers how campaigns win and lose, the state-by-state map as of mid-2026, primaries, the press, money, the courts, and what a reader can do.

- The playbook, the map, the law

> Real numbers, named opponents, and a concession wherever the other side has a point.
