## Who really elects the House

This is a chapter of Rank the Vote. It is an argument, and the "I" in it is the book's author.

Two guesses first. In 2022, what share of U.S. House seats were safe enough that the primary decided the winner? And what share of voting-age Americans voted in those primaries?

[pause]

Unite America's analysis says 83 percent of the seats, 359 of 435. And 8 percent of the voting-age population.

- 83% of House seats
- 8% of the voting-age population

> The general election is the show; the primary is the decision.

## How the machine works

In a safe district, the only election with suspense is the primary. So the only electorate the representative answers to is the primary electorate. The question that disciplines every vote is not whether the district will fire her. It is whether the primary will.

- Safe seat: the primary can fire you

Few incumbents actually lose primaries. The effect only requires every incumbent to watch the handful who did. Liz Cheney lost her 2022 Wyoming primary by 37 points.

## The honest complication

The obvious fix is to open the primaries, so that moderates win. The cleanest academic test of it came back negative. A 2014 study of state legislators found that the openness of a primary has little if any effect on the ideology of the politicians it produces.

- Open primaries: little if any effect

I read that result as the argument for bigger surgery. The defect is not who votes in the primary. It is that the primary is final. Alaska puts every candidate on one ballot, sends the top four to November regardless of party, and decides there, with ranked ballots.

- Alaska: top four advance to November

> Not opening the primary: demoting it from verdict to scrimmage.

## What it does not settle

In 2022, Senator Lisa Murkowski, censured by her own party, won Alaska's final count 53.7 to 46.3. That is one state. This chapter does not settle whether Alaska's experience generalizes, or how much moderation the bigger surgery buys.

- Open: does Alaska generalize?
