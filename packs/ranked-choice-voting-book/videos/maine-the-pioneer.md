## Thirty-seven point six

On election night in November 2010, Maine elected a governor with 37.6 percent of the vote. Paul LePage finished 9,795 votes ahead of independent Eliot Cutler. More than three of every five Maine voters had marked someone else.

- Maine 2010: a governor with 37.6%

It was not a fluke. From 1974 through 2014, Maine held eleven races for governor. In nine of them, the winner failed to reach a majority.

- 9 of 11 races: no majority winner

This is a chapter of Rank the Vote. It is an argument, and the "I" in it is the book's author.

## The voters say yes, twice

On November 8, 2016, Question 5 passed with 52.12 percent. Maine became the first state in American history to adopt ranked choice voting. Fifty-two percent is not a landslide, and I will not dress it up as one.

- Question 5, 2016: 52.12%

In October 2017 the legislature passed a bill to suspend the law, then repeal it automatically. Maine's constitution gave the voters one tool: the people's veto. They gathered over 66,000 valid signatures in a Maine winter. On June 12, 2018, the veto passed with about 54 percent.

- People's veto, 2018: about 54%

> Maine's voters approved ranked choice voting twice in nineteen months.

## Three sentences from the 1800s

Here is why this has no tidy ending. Maine's constitution says governors and legislators are elected "by a plurality of all votes returned." In May 2017 the Supreme Judicial Court advised, unanimously, that ranked choice voting conflicts with those clauses, but only where they reach.

- "By a plurality of all votes returned"

They reach general elections for governor, state senator and state representative. Primaries are not mentioned. Federal offices are governed by the U.S. Constitution.

- Ranked: primaries and federal races
- Not ranked: November races for governor, legislature

> The governor's race that started it all is still not ranked.

## The wall

In April 2026 the justices advised the same thing again, nine years later. They are probably right about the text. The fix is a constitutional amendment. It requires two-thirds of each chamber, plus a majority of voters.

- Amendment: two-thirds of each chamber, then voters

Whether ranked ballots can ever reach the governor's race is not settled. The lesson has the 2018 congressional race, where Bruce Poliquin led the first count and lost the election. It also has the federal lawsuit that followed, and the full timeline.
