## Forty-eight candidates

Don Young held Alaska's only seat in the U.S. House for 49 years. When he died in March 2022, 48 candidates filed to replace him. Under Alaska's brand-new rules they all ran on a single ballot, and the four with the most votes advanced.

- 48 candidates, one ballot, top four advance

This is a chapter of Rank the Vote. It is an argument, and the "I" in it is the book's author. Alaska is my first proving ground, and it holds the strongest point against me.

## The first count

Three candidates were on the August ballot. Democrat Mary Peltola had 40.19 percent of first choices. Republican Sarah Palin had 31.27, and Republican Nick Begich 28.53. No one had a majority, so Begich, in last place, was eliminated.

- First choices: Peltola 40.19%, Palin 31.27%, Begich 28.53%

His ballots moved to whoever each voter had ranked next: 27,053 to Palin, 15,467 to Peltola. Another 11,243 ranked no one else and exhausted.

- To Palin: 27,053
- To Peltola: 15,467
- Exhausted: 11,243

> The system did not divert Republican votes. It delivered them.

They were not enough. Peltola won the final count, 51.48 percent to 48.52.

## The critique I have to answer

Two mathematicians analyzed those ballots. They found that Begich would have beaten Peltola head-to-head, and Palin head-to-head. He was the consensus candidate, and the count eliminated him first, because he was short on first choices. This is the center squeeze, and it is real.

> The center squeeze: a consensus candidate, short on first choices, eliminated first.

I do not concede that it indicts the reform. A closed Republican primary almost certainly nominates Palin, so the old system would not have found Begich either. And we know he was the consensus candidate only because ranked ballots exist.

- Known only because ballots were ranked

## Kept by 743 votes

In 2024, opponents put repeal on the ballot. It failed by 743 votes, confirmed by a recount. On that same ballot Begich won the House seat, under the system.

- 2024 repeal failed by 743 votes

On December 31, 2025, a third measure was certified. Repeal goes back before voters in November 2026, and I will not predict the outcome.

- Repeal on the ballot again, November 2026
