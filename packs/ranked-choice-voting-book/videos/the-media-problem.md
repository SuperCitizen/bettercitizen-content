## The headline and the schedule

This is a chapter of Rank the Vote. It is an argument, and the "I" in it is the book's author.

A week before Washington, D.C.'s first ranked-choice primary, the local NBC affiliate ran this headline: "Ranked choice voting will delay DC primary results." The article itself gives the schedule. Mail ballots postmarked by election day keep arriving through June 26. That is the mail-ballot receipt deadline. It has nothing to do with ranking.

- The wait: the mail-ballot deadline

I am an advocate, and the press's job is not to make my reform look good. My complaint is narrower.

> Election coverage keeps reporting the calendar as if it were the algorithm.

## Fifteen days in Alaska

In August 2022, Alaska waited fifteen days for a result. State law gives overseas absentee ballots fifteen days to arrive. The Division of Elections had announced all along that it would run the count on August 31, and it did, on a public livestream, in minutes. A close pick-one race could not have been called before those ballots arrived either.

- 15 days: the absentee ballot window

## New York, 2021

On June 29, 2021, New York City's Board of Elections published a preliminary ranked-choice tally, then retracted it hours later. It had left about 135,000 test ballots inside the count. The error was human and institutional. Nothing about ranking caused it.

- About 135,000 test ballots

But I won't hide behind that. The rollout was a choice: a brand-new tabulation pipeline, run by the city's least-trusted agency, with preliminary results released before absentee ballots were in.

The test for a headline is one question.

> Would this sentence still be true in a pick-one race?

## What is real, and what is open

Some of the friction is structural. The elimination rounds cannot run until nearly all the ballots are in. A pollster has to simulate those rounds on a small sample, compounding error at every round. And a first-round lead can be a trap.

- Polls and calls do get harder

Better practice exists. San Francisco posts round-by-round results on election night and publishes the complete ballot file. What this chapter does not settle is whether political journalism adopts that before the next "chaos" cycle.

- San Francisco: rounds on election night
