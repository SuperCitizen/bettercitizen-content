# What Is Ranked Choice Voting?

## The Basics

Ranked choice voting (RCV) is simple: instead of picking one candidate, you rank them. First choice, second choice, third choice — as many or as few as you want.

If your first choice wins? Great, nothing changes. If your first choice is eliminated (by having the fewest votes), your vote transfers to your second choice. This continues until someone has a majority.

## It's Not New

Ranked choice voting isn't some experimental idea. It's been used in Australia since 1918. Ireland has used it since 1922. It's the standard in dozens of democracies around the world. America is the outlier, not the innovator.

## Instant Runoff vs. Multi-Winner

There are two main flavors: instant-runoff voting (IRV) for single-winner races, and single transferable vote (STV) for multi-winner races like city councils. Both use ranked ballots. Both eliminate the spoiler effect. This book covers both.
