# Constitutional Questions

## Is RCV Constitutional?

Yes. Multiple courts have upheld ranked choice voting, including federal courts. The Constitution gives states broad authority over how they conduct elections, and nothing in the Constitution requires plurality voting.

## Legal Challenges

Every major RCV implementation has faced legal challenges — and survived them. Maine's law was challenged in court and upheld. Alaska's Ballot Measure 2 has been challenged and upheld. The legal foundation is solid.

## The "One Person, One Vote" Myth

The most persistent legal myth about RCV is that it violates "one person, one vote." It doesn't. In every round of tabulation, each voter has exactly one vote. If your ballot transfers to your second choice, your first-choice vote is no longer counted. You always have one vote — it just follows your preferences.
