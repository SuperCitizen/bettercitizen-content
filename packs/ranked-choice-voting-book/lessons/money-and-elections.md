# Money and Elections

## The Cost of Runoffs

Traditional runoff elections cost millions of dollars in taxpayer money. RCV eliminates the need for runoffs entirely — the runoff happens instantly on a single ballot. Cities that have switched to RCV have saved millions.

## Campaign Finance Under RCV

When you need broad appeal instead of just base turnout, the economics of campaigning change. RCV doesn't fix money in politics by itself, but it changes the incentive structure. Candidates spend less on attack ads (which alienate potential second-choice voters) and more on positive messaging.

## The Donation Question

Some argue RCV benefits candidates with less money, since they can compete without a party machine behind them. The data supports this — RCV elections have seen more small-dollar fundraising and more competitive races between well-funded and grassroots candidates.
