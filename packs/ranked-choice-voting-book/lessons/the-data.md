# The Data

## What the Numbers Actually Say

This isn't a book of opinions — it's a book of evidence. And the evidence for ranked choice voting is overwhelming.

## Voter Turnout

Studies consistently show that RCV does not decrease voter turnout — and in many cases increases it, particularly in local elections where RCV eliminates the need for costly, low-turnout runoff elections.

## Representation

Cities and states using RCV have elected more women, more people of color, and more first-time candidates. When you don't have to survive a party primary gauntlet, a wider range of people can run — and win.

## Negative Campaigning

Academic research from multiple universities confirms what voters report anecdotally: RCV elections feature less negative campaigning. When you need your opponent's voters to rank you second, calling your opponent names is bad strategy.

## Ballot Exhaustion

Critics point to ballot exhaustion — when a voter's ranked choices are all eliminated. But ballot exhaustion rates in RCV are comparable to abstention rates in traditional runoff elections. And unlike runoffs, RCV doesn't cost taxpayers millions in additional election administration.
