# The Opposition

## Who Fights RCV and Why

Ranked choice voting has enemies, and understanding them is crucial. The opposition generally falls into three camps: those who benefit from the current system, those who genuinely misunderstand RCV, and those who've been fed misinformation.

## "It's Too Confusing"

The most common objection is that voters can't handle ranking candidates. The data says otherwise. In every jurisdiction that's implemented RCV, ballot spoilage rates are comparable to or lower than traditional elections. Voters figure it out — because it's not complicated.

## "It Helps [The Other Party]"

Both parties make this claim, which should tell you something. RCV doesn't inherently help either party. It helps candidates who appeal to the broadest coalition — which is exactly what democracy should reward.

## The Real Threat

The real reason party establishments resist RCV is simple: it threatens their gatekeeping power. When voters can rank a third-party candidate first without "wasting" their vote, the two-party duopoly weakens. For party insiders, that's terrifying.
