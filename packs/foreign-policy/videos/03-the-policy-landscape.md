## A tariff ruling

On February 20, 2026, the Supreme Court decided Learning Resources v. Trump. Since early 2025 the President had been imposing tariffs on much of the world's goods under a 1977 sanctions statute, known as IEEPA. The Court held that the statute does not authorize tariffs at all.

- February 20, 2026

Six justices agreed that a power to regulate importation is not the power to tax, which Article One gives to Congress.

- The power to tax: Congress

## What the ruling left standing

The ruling was statutory, not constitutional. Tariffs resting on statutes that delegate tariff authority expressly, Section 301 and Section 232, survived. Congress can delegate broad tariff power again, claw back what remains, or do nothing.

> The IEEPA tariffs fell. Tariffs on express delegations survived.

## Four fronts

Four fronts dominate as of mid-2026: China, Russia and Ukraine, the Middle East, and trade. Each one reduces to a lever and an owner. On trade, Congress holds the tariff power unless it delegates it. On China, the executive holds the export controls. On Ukraine, Congress appropriates the aid and the executive negotiates. In the Middle East, the lever is force.

- Trade: tariffs, Congress
- China: export controls, the executive
- Ukraine: aid, Congress appropriates
- Middle East: force, the executive in practice

## A war without a vote

On February 28, 2026, U.S. and Israeli forces opened a campaign against Iran. No congressional vote preceded it. On June 3 the House voted 215 to 208 to direct an end to hostilities, the first time a chamber has voted to halt this war.

- House, June 3, 2026: 215 to 208

One chamber is not enough.

> To bind, a measure must clear both chambers and survive a veto.

## Three schools, one pledge

Europe is re-arming under the Hague 5 percent pledge, and all three schools of the previous lesson claim it as vindication. The internationalist claims it as proof that alliances can be renegotiated. The restrainer claims it as proof that allies spend only when Washington credibly steps back. The realist claims it as the precondition for handing Europe the European problem.

- Internationalist: alliances can be renegotiated
- Restrainer: allies spend when Washington steps back
- Realist: Europe carries the European problem

## A moving target

This is a snapshot, and the facts will age. The pattern is the durable part: instruments that require Congress move slowly and bind durably, and instruments the executive holds alone move fast and reverse fast.

> What needs Congress binds durably. What the executive holds alone reverses fast.

The lesson takes all three schools through each front, from Taiwan to the Strait of Hormuz, and names what to watch on each.
