## Fourteen bombs, no vote

On June 21, 2025, fourteen bunker-buster bombs struck Iran's nuclear-enrichment sites at Fordow and Natanz. The President announced the operation that evening, and most members of Congress learned of it the way everyone else did.

- June 21, 2025

No congressional vote preceded the strikes, and none followed. Six days later the Senate declined, 47 to 53, to advance a resolution requiring one.

- Senate: 47 to 53

## What the text says

Now hold that scene against the text. Article One gives Congress, not the President, the power "to declare War." Congress has declared war eleven times across five conflicts, and not once since June 1942, during the Second World War.

- Last declaration of war: June 1942

Korea, Vietnam, the Gulf War, Afghanistan and Iraq all went ahead without one. Presidents of both parties have used force on their own authority hundreds of times.

> The text gives Congress the war decision. Practice has run the other way.

## A clock nobody starts

In 1973 Congress passed the War Powers Resolution over Nixon's veto. The President must report within 48 hours of sending forces into hostilities, and must end the operation within 60 days unless Congress authorizes it.

- Report within 48 hours
- End within 60 days, unless authorized

Every administration, of both parties, has filed the reports while maintaining that the binding provisions are unconstitutional. Congress has never once used the Resolution to force a withdrawal. After the Iran strikes, the statute produced a report, a floor vote, and no constraint.

> The statute produced a report, a floor vote, and no constraint.

## Promises that last, and promises that do not

A treaty needs the consent of two-thirds of the Senate. That bar is so demanding that treaties make up less than 7 percent of U.S. international agreements since the World War Two era.

- Treaty: two-thirds of the Senate

A commitment that rests on the President's signature alone is fast to make, and the next President can walk away from it. The Paris climate agreement and the Iran nuclear deal were both built that way, and each was unwound by a successor.

- Signature alone: the next President can exit

> Durability tracks the approval hurdle.

## Not yet a position

In 2025 the civilian instruments were reorganized in a single year: supporters say rationalized, critics say gutted. Whether the United States should run an alliance-heavy, forward-deployed foreign policy is the contested question, and this lesson does not answer it.

- The levers, not where to point them

The lesson maps the rest of the machine: the 2001 authorization still in force as of mid-2026, an alliance network of 32 NATO members and more, and sanctions, aid and defense dollars.
