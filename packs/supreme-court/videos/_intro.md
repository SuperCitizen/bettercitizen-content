## Nine people and a closed door

On conference days, nine people walk into a room and close the door. No clerks, and no recording. What they decide there can void what elected majorities enact.

> Why should nine unelected lawyers void what elected majorities enact?

## Three lessons

The first lesson shows how the Court works: the funnel that turns thousands of petitions into a few dozen argued cases, the private conference, and the fast-growing emergency docket.

- 1. How the Court works

The second goes back to Marbury v. Madison, the case that gave the Court its power, and to three schools of thought on how unelected judges should use it.

- 2. Judicial review and its critics

The third covers the proposals to change the Court itself: term limits, expansion, jurisdiction stripping, and a binding ethics code. Each position gets its strongest case, and none is declared right.

- 3. The reform debate

## Where to start

Start with the first lesson, How the Supreme Court actually works. It begins at that closed door.
