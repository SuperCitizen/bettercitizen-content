## The safety mechanism

In January 2010, Justice Kennedy described what made the rest of Citizens United tolerable to its own majority: prompt disclosure, so that citizens can see whether elected officials are "in the pocket" of moneyed interests. Eight Justices upheld the disclosure rules.

Fifteen years later, the 2024 elections ran on a record 1.9 billion dollars whose original sources no citizen can see.

- 2010: disclosure upheld 8–1
- 2024: $1.9 billion, sources unseen

## One phrase

Under the FEC's regulation, an outside group discloses only contributions "made for the purpose of furthering" the specific reported ad. A donor avoids that by simply not earmarking. Give to a nonprofit for its "general purposes," and no federal filing will ever connect your name to the ads it buys.

> An unearmarked gift to a nonprofit never surfaces in a federal filing.

## The reformer case

Reformers argue the Constitution already said yes. Citizens United upheld disclosure 8 to 1. Months later Doe v. Reed upheld disclosure of petition signers, again 8 to 1. The DISCLOSE Act passed the House in 2010 and died by filibuster. They point to Wood and Spencer's study, which found the chilling effect negligible: on average, less than one donor per candidate stops giving.

- Citizens United and Doe v. Reed: 8–1
- Wood and Spencer: negligible chill

> The reformer case: the obstacle is the Senate, not the First Amendment.

## The donor-privacy case

Privacy advocates argue from NAACP v. Alabama, in 1958. Alabama demanded the NAACP's membership lists, and a unanimous Court refused: identified members faced reprisal, threats and violence. They point to Americans for Prosperity Foundation v. Bonta, in 2021, which requires narrow tailoring for any compelled donor disclosure. They answer the study by pointing to the rare but severe cases, not the average.

- NAACP v. Alabama, 1958: unanimous
- Bonta, 2021: narrow tailoring

> The donor-privacy case: disclosure converts giving into a target list.

## Thomas's lonely square

Justice Thomas, alone in 2010, rejects the compromise both camps accept. He argues that disclosure itself chills speech, and points to a tradition that prized anonymous political speech. It remains a minority position, though Bonta moved the median Justice toward it.

- Thomas: disclosure itself chills speech

## What is not settled

Where the NAACP's protection ends and the electorate's right to know begins is not settled, and the Court that draws that line is not the Court of 2010.

- The line will be relitigated
