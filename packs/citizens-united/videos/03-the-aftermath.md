## One donor

In 2008, the last federal election held before Citizens United, every outside group in the country combined spent 338 million dollars. In 2024, one donor spent 290 million dollars by himself.

- 2008, all outside groups: $338 million
- 2024, one donor: $290 million

Every part of that was lawful, and no part of it was possible fifteen years earlier. The 2010 ruling alone did not produce it.

## The Super PAC

Citizens United said nothing about what a group could collect. Two months later, in SpeechNow.org v. FEC, the full D.C. Circuit finished the syllogism. If independent spending cannot corrupt, then contributions to a group that does nothing but that spending cannot corrupt either. The cap fell.

The same opinion upheld the registration and reporting requirements. SpeechNow had to organize as a political committee and itemize its donors publicly.

- SpeechNow.org v. FEC, March 2010
- Donor reporting upheld

> Citizens United opened the spending. SpeechNow opened the fundraising.

## The wall

On paper, the wall between a Super PAC and a campaign is what makes the structure constitutional. In practice it has been engineered around.

Take redboxing. A campaign posts message scripts and voter-targeting guidance on a public webpage, and the allied Super PAC executes it. The coordination rules turn on privately conveyed, nonpublic information. A public webpage is by definition not nonpublic.

- Redboxing: public scripts, lawful use

> The coordination rules reach private information, and redboxing is public.

## The referee

The FEC needs four votes to investigate, to make rules or to find a violation. On May 1, 2025, it dropped below its four-member quorum. Two replacements were nominated in February 2026. As of mid-2026 the Senate had not confirmed them.

- May 1, 2025: no quorum
- Mid-2026: nominees unconfirmed

[check q4]

## Two readings

Two honest readings coexist. The maximalist reading: a weak FEC is a feature, because an aggressive one would be a weapon incumbents aim at challengers. The reformer reading: the regime the Court described in 2010, vigorous disclosure and enforced independence, has never existed since.

- Maximalist: a weak FEC is a feature
- Reformer: the assumed regime never existed

> Both accounts fit the record. They disagree about what it means.
