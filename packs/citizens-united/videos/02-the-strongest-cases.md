## Two sentences, one day

Two sentences from one day in 2010 are battle standards still. Justice Kennedy, for the majority, wrote of government using "censorship to control thought." Justice Stevens, in dissent, wrote that "few outside the majority of this Court would have thought its flaws included a dearth of corporate money in politics."

Each is devastating against a weak version of the other side. Neither touches the strong version.

- Kennedy, for the majority
- Stevens, in dissent

## The First Amendment maximalist case

This position starts from a categorical text: "Congress shall make no law … abridging the freedom of speech." Its strongest version is not "money is speech." It is that the means of amplification are inseparable from the speech itself. "You may speak, but you may not spend to be heard" hollows the right for every speaker who is not already famous.

What would change it: documented quid pro quo corruption, at scale, from genuinely independent spending.

> The maximalist case: the means of amplification are inseparable from speech.

## The anti-corruption case

This position starts by refusing the premise that independent means independent. Super PACs in practice are founded by candidates' former aides and serve a single candidate. Its strongest version names a harm with no bribe in it, dependence corruption: representatives dependent on the funders rather than on the people alone.

What would change it: a showing of better-informed voters, and no measurable shift in access or agenda, since 2010.

> The anti-corruption case: dependence on funders corrupts without any bribe.

## The democratic-equality case

This position starts earlier, with Buckley v. Valeo in 1976. The anti-corruption advocate accepts Buckley and says Citizens United misapplied it. This position says Buckley itself is the error. Its strongest version holds that political equality is itself a constitutional value: citizens' political influence should not scale with their wealth.

What would change it: a showing that equality-motivated rules in practice entrench incumbents.

> The democratic-equality case: political influence should not scale with wealth.

## A fourth voice

The institutionalists refuse all three framings. La Raja and Schaffner argue that capping party money backfired. Money rerouted from party committees to ideological outside groups and purist donors. Their cure is to deregulate the parties, not to re-cap the outside groups.

What would change it: evidence that party-centered finance does not moderate.

> The institutionalist case: limits rerouted money from parties to purists.

## What the evidence settles

Settled: independent spending has grown enormously since 2010. Contested: whether it changes electoral outcomes, and whether dependence corruption is real and measurable.

- Settled: independent spending grew
- Contested: outcomes, dependence corruption

The lesson gives each position its full scaffolding, then asks you to pick a frame and name the evidence that would change your mind.
