## Heard as its adherents hear it

The hardest thing about understanding any ideology, including your own, is hearing it as its adherents hear it, not as opponents caricature it. This pack does that for American conservatism.

> Conservatism is a family of positions, not a creed.

## Three lessons

The first lesson gives the commitments most conservatives share, the thinkers behind them, and the postwar coalition that joined three streams and later came apart.

- 1. What American conservatism actually believes

The second takes the three streams of today: traditionalist, classical liberal and national conservative. Each one makes its strongest case against the other two, and none is declared the winner.

- 2. Internal disagreements within the tradition

The third maps the borders with progressivism, libertarianism and communitarianism, and sets American conservatism beside the European kind.

- 3. How conservatism relates to other traditions

## Where to start

Start with the first lesson, What American conservatism actually believes. It opens in 1955, with a new magazine and a dare.

- Start with lesson 1
