## A socialist and a conservative

In February 2025, the Senate's only democratic socialist and one of its loudest national conservatives introduced a bill together. Bernie Sanders and Josh Hawley proposed a hard federal ceiling on credit-card interest.

- Sanders and Hawley, February 2025
- A 10% cap on card interest

That is a paradox only if the American left-right axis is as wide as political rhetoric suggests. The lesson argues it is more compressed than that.

## The border with progressivism

Conservatism and progressivism share a surprising amount. Traditionalists and progressives both criticize pure individualism. National conservatives and pro-labor progressives share concern for the working class. And populist conservatives and progressives are both skeptical of unaccountable expertise. Conservatives focus on universities and bureaucratic agencies. Progressives focus on corporate power and Wall Street.

- Shared: skepticism of unaccountable power
- Different targets

What divides them is inherited institutions, such as family structures and religious communities. Progressivism generally treats them as objects of critique and reform. Conservatism generally treats them as objects of conservation.

> Reform the inherited institutions, or conserve them: that is the main divide.

## Same vote, different reasons

A century-old lodge system is dying. A libertarian and a Burkean conservative both oppose federal money to rescue it. The libertarian values the lodges only as far as people freely choose them. The Burkean thinks their accumulated practice has worth apart from anyone's choice, and counts the loss as a real cost.

- Libertarian: worth comes from free choice
- Burkean: worth beyond current choices

> Identical votes can conceal opposite premises.

## The American outlier

European conservative parties, such as Germany's Christian Democrats, generally accept large welfare states and are comfortable with industrial policy. American conservatism has had a free-market default since the fusionist era.

- Europe: accepts large welfare states
- America: free-market default

> What Americans call conservatism is, in several respects, the historical outlier.

National conservatives in the United States often draw on those European traditions deliberately.

## Borders, not battle lines

Shared bills are not shared philosophy. The lesson says the policy landscape is more cross-cut than the partisan one, and that agreement in substance is not always agreement in votes.

- Convergence on policy, not philosophy

The lesson maps each border in turn, including communitarianism, and lists where left and right now converge, from Big Tech to trade with China. It leaves open whether those convergences are durable realignments or marriages of convenience.
