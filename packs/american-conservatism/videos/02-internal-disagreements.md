## One of their own

On May 29, 2019, Sohrab Ahmari published an essay titled "Against David French-ism." Its target was not a progressive. David French was a National Review writer, an evangelical, and a religious-liberty litigator with a long pro-life record.

- Ahmari against French, 2019

Ahmari argued that French's civil, procedural conservatism was unilateral disarmament in a culture war. The conservative world spent that summer arguing about one of its own.

> The bitterest arguments often run within left and right, not between them.

## The traditionalist stream

Conservatism has three major streams. The traditionalist stream is committed to cultural and religious continuity. It argues that markets and state power can both hollow out a community, and that the real work is patient rebuilding, close to the parish, the family and the local school.

- Core: cultural and religious continuity

> The traditionalist case: the state is too blunt an instrument for cultural renewal.

## The classical-liberal stream

The classical-liberal stream is committed to limited government, individual liberty, free markets and the rule of law. It argues that institutions are best conserved by free people in free markets and free associations, and that tariffs and industrial policy will be captured by the well-connected.

- Core: limited government, free markets

> The classical-liberal case: a tradition that needs the state's scaffolding has already died.

## The national-conservative stream

The national-conservative stream is committed to the political community as a unit: the nation, its families and its industrial base. It argues that free-market orthodoxy cost the working class its towns and wages, and that government may act to support family and industry.

- Core: the political community as a unit

> The national-conservative case: cultural preservation requires political power.

## Where each one lands

Traditionalists and national conservatives largely share a cultural diagnosis. They split over the instrument: winning and using state power, or rebuilding civil society from below.

- Same diagnosis, different instrument

Now put a tariff in front of all three. National conservatives generally support it. Classical liberals are strongly opposed. Traditionalists are skeptical, because it is state action. The same split holds for industrial policy.

- Tariffs: national conservatives generally support
- Classical liberals: strongly opposed
- Traditionalists: skeptical

## No new fusion yet

The current Republican coalition is a contested mixture of all three streams, plus populist elements that fit none of them cleanly.

- A contested mixture, not a synthesis

The lesson lets each stream make its case against the other two, and shows where each lands on nine issues. It does not settle whether a new synthesis can hold them together, or whether one stream simply wins.
