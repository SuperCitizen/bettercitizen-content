## A dare in 1955

In 1950 the critic Lionel Trilling wrote that liberalism was the sole intellectual tradition in the United States. Conservative impulses, he wrote, came out as "irritable mental gestures which seek to resemble ideas."

Five years later, on November 19, 1955, William F. Buckley Jr. published the first issue of National Review. The magazine, he wrote, "stands athwart history, yelling Stop."

- National Review, 1955

Within a generation, the movement that magazine assembled had elected Ronald Reagan.

## What the family shares

Conservatism is a family of positions, not a creed. Most of its members share a core. Order comes before freedom, because liberty is fragile without working institutions. Government is necessary but dangerous. And the family, the congregation and the local association do most of the work of social order.

- Order before freedom
- Limits to state power
- Family and civil society first

So the test of a program is not whether government acts. A program that strengthens the family can be conservative. One that takes over the family's work is not.

> The state's role is to support family and civil society, not displace them.

## Burke's inheritance

The tradition's intellectual father is Edmund Burke. His Reflections on the Revolution in France, in 1790, made the classic case against politics by first principles. It defended the slow work of inherited institutions.

- Burke, Reflections, 1790

Burke was not against change. He was skeptical of root-and-branch change.

> Institutions refined by generations hold knowledge that abstract reasoning cannot reproduce.

## Three streams, fused

After the Second World War, three streams joined that sat in tension. Traditionalists cared about cultural inheritance. Free-market classical liberals cared about economic freedom and limited government. Anti-communists cared about the threat of the Soviet Union, the unifying enemy.

- Traditionalists
- Free-market classical liberals
- Anti-communists

The blend was called fusionism.

> American conservatism was a coalition from birth.

[check q2]

## After the Soviet Union

Fusionism was built for a world with a Soviet Union in it. The Soviet collapse in 1991 removed the unifying enemy. In 2016 Donald Trump won the nomination against the fusionist establishment, and then the presidency.

- 1991: the unifying enemy disappears
- 2016: the realignment

## Whose inheritance?

Traditionalists emphasize cultural and religious continuity, family and community. Free-market classical liberals emphasize economic freedom, limited government and the rule of law. Civic-republican, or national, conservatives emphasize the political community as a unit, and state action to support family, industry and virtue.

- Traditionalists: continuity and community
- Classical liberals: economic freedom, limited government
- National conservatives: the political community

The lesson does not settle which stream has the better claim. It gives you the thinkers one by one, and the road from Goldwater's defeat to Reagan's win.
