## Chicago's first school board vote

On November 5, 2024, Chicagoans did something their city had never done in 187 years. They elected school board members: ten of them, to sit beside eleven the mayor still appointed.

[beat]

One of the country's biggest cities is only now adopting something that some 13,300 other communities have had all along.

- 13,318 school districts
- Roughly 90,000 board members

> The most common elected government in America is also the least examined.

## The layer cake

Most public schools are not run by city hall. They are run by an independent school district: a separate government with its own elected board and its own budget.

But the board's power is lent to it by the state. The state legislature sets academic standards, graduation requirements and testing, and it can merge or take over a district.

- State: standards, graduation, testing

The federal government has never run schools or set course content. It works through money and civil-rights law. Its funding obligates districts to educate students with disabilities.

- Federal: money and civil-rights law

What the board itself holds is district policy and the budget. So a candidate who promises to abolish the state's standardized tests is promising something a board majority cannot deliver. A candidate who promises to move budget dollars into classroom aides is promising something it can.

- Board: district policy and the budget

> Sort each promise into its layer before you judge the candidate.

## One employee

A school board employs exactly one person: the superintendent. It hires that person, and can fire that person. It adopts the budget and sets district policy.

- The board's one employee: the superintendent

The superintendent runs the system day to day, and hires and supervises the principals.

Researchers consistently find that effective boards keep to goals, policy and oversight. Troubled boards micromanage operations, until no one can say who is accountable for results.

- Failure mode: micromanaging

> The board governs. The superintendent manages.

## Who chooses the board

More than 90 percent of board members are elected, very often on dates with nothing federal on the ballot. The National School Boards Association's own estimate is that turnout typically runs 5 to 10 percent.

- Turnout: 5 to 10%

Whether elected or appointed boards produce better schools, and whether moving the election date would help, are questions on which serious people disagree.
