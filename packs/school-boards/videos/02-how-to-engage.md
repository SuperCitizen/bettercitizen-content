## The document nobody opens

Some time before your school board's next regular meeting, a document will appear on the district website that almost nobody opens. It is the agenda packet: dozens or hundreds of pages of staff reports, budget transfers, contracts and policies.

- The agenda packet

Every consequential decision the board will make that night is previewed there in writing, days in advance, by law.

> Open-government law gives the public a head start on its school board.

## Before the vote, not after

The parents who pack the boardroom after a school closure is approved are reacting to a decision that was legible in a document weeks earlier. Usually twice, since most policies get two readings.

- Most policies get two readings

The board's year has a calendar too. If your issue is money, the moment is the winter budget workshops, when line items are still in play. By the adoption vote the text is set, and speeches are for the record.

- Money: winter budget workshops
- Adoption vote: text already set

> The earlier the stage, the smaller the audience, and the more one voice weighs.

## The right door

The board sits at the top of the school system's ladder, so most concerns should not start there. It governs policy and employs one person, the superintendent.

A grade, or a conflict with a teacher, starts with the teacher, and then the principal. Special-education services for your child start with the case manager, and then the district special-education office.

- Your child: teacher, then principal
- Special education: case manager first

Districtwide policy is the board's actual job: the discipline code, library policy, calendars. State testing mandates belong to your state legislator.

- Districtwide policy: the board
- State mandates: the legislature

## Early beats loud

Two places outrank the lectern for lasting influence. One is the parent organizations, which give you allies and information. The other is the advisory committees, where draft policy is shaped months before a board vote. The lesson's measure: a seat on the bond-oversight committee is worth a year of three-minute comments.

- Advisory and oversight committees

> Organized and early beats eloquent and late.

[check q4]

## What it cannot promise

None of this guarantees a win. State mandates, federal obligations and multi-year contracts leave a board perhaps a few discretionary percentage points to fight over. And the lesson leaves one question open: what should be done about an election that 5 to 10 percent of voters decide.

- 5 to 10% turnout

The lesson has the three look-ups that find your district, the rules for closed sessions, and the arithmetic of a recall against a regular election.
