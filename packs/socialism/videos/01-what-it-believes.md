## A candidate in a prison cell

On November 2, 1920, more than nine hundred thousand Americans voted for a presidential candidate in a federal prison cell. Eugene V. Debs was serving a ten-year sentence for a speech against the World War One draft.

- Debs, 1920: 914,191 votes

Three years earlier, the Bolsheviks had seized power in Russia and called what they were building socialism. The Americans who voted for Debs overwhelmingly wanted something else: the eight-hour day, old-age insurance, and the ballot as the way to get it.

> The same word covered both projects then, and it covers both now.

## One claim, two questions

Socialism makes one core claim: the major productive assets of an economy should be socially owned or controlled, not held as private capital.

- Core claim: social ownership of production

Everything that divides socialists lives inside two questions. How much of the economy should be socially owned? And by what means: ballots and parliaments, or a revolutionary party that does not intend to share power?

- How much social ownership?
- By what political means?

## Four families

Marxist-Leninist state socialism combined comprehensive state ownership and central planning with single-party rule. Democratic socialism wants substantial social ownership, achieved and maintained only through democratic politics.

- State socialism: single-party rule
- Democratic socialism: democratic politics only

Social democracy accepts a mostly private market economy, and uses taxes, universal services and strong unions to civilize it. The Nordic model is social democracy in practice. Denmark's prime minister drew the line himself in 2015: "Denmark is a market economy."

- Social democracy: a civilized market economy
- Nordic model: social democracy in practice

## Reform or revolution

In 1899 Eduard Bernstein argued that capitalism was not collapsing, and that socialism would arrive, if it arrived, through democratic suffrage, labor organization and patient reform.

- Bernstein: the ballot and patient reform

Rosa Luxemburg argued in reply that reforms were worth fighting for, but could never transform the system that conceded them.

- Luxemburg: reform cannot transform the system

Then, in 1917, Lenin's Bolsheviks took power and built a one-party state. By the early 1920s every country's left was split into a Communist branch and a social-democratic branch.
