## A word wins New York

On the night of November 4, 2025, a member of the Democratic Socialists of America was elected mayor of New York City, with 50.8 percent of the vote. A word that ended American careers for seventy years was now attached to the chief executive of the country's largest city.

- Zohran Mamdani: 50.8%

Both parties spent the next year arguing about what, exactly, had won.

## Five planks, one test

Mamdani ran on affordability: a rent freeze, fare-free buses, universal childcare, five city-owned grocery stores, and a higher minimum wage.

Almost none of this transfers the means of production. The buses and childcare are services, free at the point of use. The rent freeze and the minimum wage regulate private markets. Only the grocery stores put government into ownership, as a pilot.

- Free services: buses, childcare
- Regulation: rent freeze, minimum wage
- Social ownership: five grocery stores

> Only the grocery stores put production in public hands.

## Two readings

As of mid-2026 the record is mixed: one delivery, one stall, and two pendings. One reading calls that vindication: material wins, delivered democratically. The other calls it dilution: social democracy with a bolder label.

- Vindication: wins, delivered democratically
- Dilution: social democracy, bolder label

## The neighbors

Progressives want to regulate and tax private capitalism into fairness. Socialists locate the problem in ownership itself.

- Progressivism: divided by ownership

With conservatism, socialism shares a reflex to protect society from the market. The two divide over who counts as the society being protected.

- Conservatism: divided by who "we" are

With libertarianism the border is a cliff: the status of property.

- Libertarianism: divided by property

> The bills can match while the destinations differ.

## The Nordic mirror

Every American argument about socialism eventually invokes Scandinavia. Denmark collects 45.2 percent of GDP in taxes, and the United States 25.6. But not one Nordic country has a statutory minimum wage. Wage floors are set through collective bargaining, backed by union density five to six times the U.S. rate.

- Denmark: taxes 45.2% of GDP
- United States: 25.6%
- Nordic statutory minimum wage: none

The lesson's conclusion cuts at everyone: what the Nordics built is a social democracy, atop private ownership.
