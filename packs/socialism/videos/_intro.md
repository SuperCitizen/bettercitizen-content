## One word, two projects

In 1920, more than nine hundred thousand Americans voted for a socialist who was in a federal prison cell. Three years earlier, the Bolsheviks had seized power in Russia and called their project socialism too. In American arguments now, the same word covers both Bernie Sanders and the Soviet Union.

- One word, more than one project

> Until you can take the word apart, you cannot think clearly about it.

## Three lessons

The first lesson takes the word apart: its core claim, and the four families that claim sorts into.

- 1. What socialism actually believes

The second gives the tradition's critics their full say, and then the three streams of socialists arguing with one another.

- 2. Disagreements and the strongest critiques

The third sets socialism beside its neighbors, from progressivism to libertarianism, and follows a democratic socialist governing New York City.

- 3. Socialism and other traditions

The tradition is stated as its adherents would state it, and the critiques at full force.
