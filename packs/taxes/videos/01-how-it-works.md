## The raise nobody should refuse

Ask around any office in December and you will meet someone who turned down a raise. They say it would have pushed them into the next bracket, and they would take home less.

[beat]

They declined free money, to dodge a rule that does not exist.

> No raise can lower your take-home pay through the brackets.

## The staircase

The United States does not tax your income at one rate. It taxes slices of it, at rising rates, like a staircase.

- Slices of income, not one rate

Climbing to a higher step changes the tax on the dollars on that step. It never changes the dollars below.

Take a single filer on a salary of 100,000 dollars in 2026. Her top step is the 22 percent bracket. Her total bill is 13,170 dollars, which is 13.2 percent of her salary.

- Marginal rate: 22%
- Effective rate: 13.2%

> Your marginal rate is the next dollar. Your effective rate is the average.

## Where the money comes from

In fiscal year 2025 the federal government collected about 5.23 trillion dollars. Guess how much came from the income tax, and how much from the taxes on your pay stub.

[pause]

About 51 cents of each dollar came from the individual income tax. About 33 came from payroll taxes, which fund Social Security and Medicare. The corporate income tax brought in about 9, and tariffs about 4.

- Income tax: 51%
- Payroll taxes: 33%
- Corporate tax: 9%
- Tariffs: 4%

> Payroll taxes are enormous, and most tax debates leave them out.

## The question behind every tax

Who writes the check is not the whole story. Economists ask who actually bears a tax, and for some taxes the answer is disputed.

- Who bears it, not who pays it
