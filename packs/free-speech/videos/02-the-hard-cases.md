## A thousand feet from a funeral

In March 2006, Albert Snyder buried his son Matthew, a twenty-year-old Marine killed in Iraq. A thousand feet from the funeral, members of the Westboro Baptist Church held signs reading "Thank God for Dead Soldiers."

A jury awarded the grieving father millions. In 2011, in Snyder v. Phelps, the Supreme Court took the verdict away, eight to one.

- Snyder v. Phelps, 2011
- Verdict taken away, 8 to 1

## The category that is not there

American law keeps a closed list of unprotected categories of speech, such as threats and fighting words. Hate speech is not on it.

In 1990, teenagers burned a cross inside the fenced yard of a Black family in St. Paul, Minnesota. The city charged one under an ordinance aimed at symbols that arouse anger on the basis of race or religion. In R.A.V. v. City of St. Paul, in 1992, all nine justices agreed the ordinance was unconstitutional.

- St. Paul's ordinance: unconstitutional, all nine

The majority conceded that the burning cross could be punished as a threat or as fighting words. It could not be punished under a law that singled out racial and religious invective and left every other insult legal. Even inside an unprotected category, government may not pick viewpoints.

- Inside a category, no picking viewpoints

> Hateful speech is punished only when it meets one of the category tests.

## The opposite choice

Canada's Supreme Court upheld the crime of wilfully promoting hatred. Germany criminalizes incitement to hatred. Which system has it right is a contested question, and the lesson gives three positions on it.

> Nearly every other major democracy made the opposite choice.

## Three positions

The civil-libertarian absolutist argues that the fatal question is who decides. Definitions of hatred are written by whoever holds power, and a government that can punish the Klan's ideas today can punish a liberation movement's tomorrow.

- Absolutist: neutrality shields minorities

The dignity-and-equality school argues that hate propaganda tells a target group that society's guarantee of equal membership does not cover them. That loads the cost of free speech onto its most vulnerable beneficiaries.

- Dignity school: the vulnerable bear the cost

The contextual balancer argues that both camps overstate. Targeted harassment, threats and incitement are already punishable, and judged case by case, the labels differ more than the outcomes.

- Balancer: labels differ more than outcomes
