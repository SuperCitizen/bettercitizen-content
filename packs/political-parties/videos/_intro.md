## A warning nobody heeded

George Washington warned the country against the spirit of party. His own cabinet was already splitting into two of them. The Constitution has no political parties in it, and by 1796 the country had them anyway.

> Washington warned against parties while his own cabinet was splitting into them.

The argument about them has never stopped.

## Three lessons

The first lesson says what a party actually is, which turns out to be three different things. It shows the real org chart, the money that now dwarfs it, and why there are exactly two.

- 1. What parties actually are

The second gives the strongest case for each of three views: that parties should be stronger, that they should be weaker, and that the trouble is weak parties joined to strong partisanship. None is declared right.

- 2. The strongest cases

The third walks the reforms on the table: primary rules, fusion voting, proportional representation, party finance, and the parties' own internal democracy.

- 3. The reform landscape
