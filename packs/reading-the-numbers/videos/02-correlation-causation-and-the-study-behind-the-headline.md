## Up or down?

Did violent crime in America rise or fall in 2022? The FBI's statistics, assembled from police agencies, showed it falling about 2 percent. A federal survey that asks Americans directly whether they were attacked showed it rising.

- FBI: down about 2%
- Victimization survey: up

Neither is lying. One counts crimes that were reported to police and passed along. The other asks households, and so includes crimes nobody reported.

> Every statistic is made by a procedure, and the procedure decides its meaning.

## The ladder of evidence

When a claim says a policy caused something, ask what design produced the numbers. An anecdote shows that something is possible, never that it is typical. A cross-section compares many places at one moment. But the places differ in a thousand ways besides the policy, and there is no time order.

- Anecdote: possible, not typical
- Cross-section: many differences, no time order

A panel follows the same places across years. A natural experiment lets history assign the policy. At the top sits the randomized trial: assignment by coin flip.

- Panel, natural experiment, randomized trial

In 2006, researchers randomly assigned about 180,000 Michigan households to receive different mailers. The one showing a household its neighbors' voting records raised turnout by 8.1 points. The only systematic difference between the groups was the envelope.

- Neighbors mailer: turnout up 8.1 points

> Design determines how much the word "because" can carry.

## The fake testimonial

Policy targets extremes. The crackdown follows the worst crime spike. The turnaround program goes to the lowest-scoring schools. But an extreme year is partly luck, and luck does not repeat. Next year the spike subsides and the scores tick up, as they mostly would have anyway.

- Regression to the mean

> Every program aimed at an extreme is born with a fake testimonial.

The test is a comparison group, selected the same extreme way and left untreated.

- Test: an untreated comparison group

## Where the ladder runs out

The ladder ranks confidence about cause, not importance. No one will randomize democracy, war or a border. The lesson gives three positions on what follows.

The credibility-revolution advocate argues that where the design is dirty, the scientific position is to say "we don't know" out loud.

- Advocate: say "we don't know"

The structural-model defender argues that natural experiments answer only the questions nature happened to randomize.

- Defender: clean designs answer narrow questions

The evidence-consumer pragmatist argues that public decisions cannot wait for ideal evidence, so the skill is grading each study by its rung.

- Pragmatist: grade each study by rung

The lesson gives each its case, along with what a p-value says, the replication reckoning, and three checks for the study behind any headline.
