## Two point four million ballots

In 1936 a magazine, The Literary Digest, mailed about ten million straw ballots and got roughly 2.4 million back. Its verdict: Alf Landon over Franklin Roosevelt. Roosevelt took 60.8 percent of the popular vote.

- 2.4 million ballots returned
- Missed by 19 points

[beat]

The Digest's lists skewed prosperous, and Landon's supporters mailed their ballots back at far higher rates.

> How you select people determines what your sample can say.

## One spoonful

A cook judges the pot from one spoonful. Not because the spoon is large, but because the pot is stirred. Random selection is the stirring.

If every member of a population has a known chance of landing in the sample, about 1,000 people estimate a percentage for the whole country within about 3 points, 95 times out of 100.

- 1,000 people: about ±3 points

And the size of the population barely matters. A random sample of 1,000 reads the United States about as precisely as it reads Wyoming.

> Precision comes from the size of the sample, not the size of the country.

[check q1]

## What the margin leaves out

The margin of error covers sampling error only: the bad luck in who the random draw happened to reach. Four other kinds of error never appear in it.

The list of people who could be sampled is not the population. The people who answer differ from the people who do not.

- Coverage error
- Nonresponse error

Question wording shapes the answers. And weighting repairs only the skews a pollster can name.

- Measurement error
- Adjustment error

So a poll can miss by more than its margin and nobody lied.

> The margin of error is a floor on uncertainty, never a ceiling.

[check q2]

## Three readings of the record

The field audits itself after each election. In 2024, state presidential polls had their most accurate cycle since 1944. And for the third presidential cycle in a row, the misses pointed the same way.

Serious people hold three readings of that record. The survey-methods institutionalist argues that the system is working: the field publishes its own errors, and they are managed risk.

- Institutionalist: errors audited and managed

The structural polling skeptic argues that polls now measure the kind of person who answers, a problem no weight can cure.

- Skeptic: a problem weighting cannot cure

The aggregation pragmatist argues that no single poll is news, and that averages with error bands are the proper unit of belief.

- Pragmatist: averages with error bands

The data so far refuses to retire any of them. The lesson gives each its case, with the record of 2016, 2020 and 2024, and the arithmetic of herding, exit polls and crosstabs.
