## Few and defined

In 1788 James Madison promised that the powers of the new federal government would be "few and defined," and that those left to the states would be "numerous and indefinite."

[beat]

The Constitution divides power between Washington and the states. That division has been tested ever since.

> How far does a named power reach, and who decides?

## Two lessons

The first lesson asks who decides, Washington or your state. It sets the list of powers in Article One beside the Tenth Amendment, then follows the cases that drew the lines, from McCulloch v. Maryland to Wickard v. Filburn.

- 1. Who decides, Washington or your state?

The second asks whether Washington can make a state go along. It covers what happens when Washington pays a state, when it tries to order one, and when a state resists.

- 2. Can Washington make a state go along?

## Where to start

Start with the first lesson, Who decides, Washington or your state? It begins with thirteen soccer clubs and a charter that both sides are reading.

- Start with lesson 1
