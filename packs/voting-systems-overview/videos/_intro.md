## Who won?

In 2009, Burlington, Vermont elected a mayor, and then spent a year arguing about who had actually won. One stack of ballots gave three defensible winners, depending entirely on the counting rule.

> One stack of ballots, three defensible winners.

## Two lessons

The first lesson shows how five systems work: plurality, ranked-choice, approval, score and STAR. For each, what the ballot asks, how the count proceeds, and where it is used as of mid-2026.

- 1. The five systems

The second weighs the tradeoffs, from Arrow's theorem to the evidence on turnout and ballot errors. It does not pick a winner.

- 2. Tradeoffs and evidence

This is the neutral counterpart to the two advocacy books in this library.

## Where to start

Start with the first lesson, The five voting systems most people argue about. You can argue about tradeoffs only after you can describe each system.

- Start with lesson 1
