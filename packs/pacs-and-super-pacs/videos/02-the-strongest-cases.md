## Two checks

In January 2012, Newt Gingrich's presidential campaign was broke and presumed dead. Then Sheldon Adelson wrote a 5 million dollar check to the super PAC supporting him, and Gingrich won South Carolina. The family's support eventually reached about 21 million dollars.

- Adelson family: about $21 million

> Two people lawfully kept a major presidential candidacy alive.

Read one way, that is the First Amendment working. Read another way, candidates survive by acquiring a billionaire. Three camps argue over which.

## The PAC-defender

The PAC-defender argues that effective political speech costs money, so a cap on political spending is a cap on political speech. A super PAC is citizens pooling resources to say something together. The defender points to hydraulics: blocked money reroutes, it does not dry up. The 1974 limits produced PACs, and the 2002 soft-money ban produced 527 groups. The super PAC, at least, names its donors.

- Hydraulics: blocked money reroutes

> The defender: restrict disclosed money, and it flows somewhere darker.

[check q3]

## The PAC-restrictionist

The PAC-restrictionist argues that the harm is dependence, not bribery. When a viable candidacy requires the patronage of the largest funders, officials answer to those funders, and no envelope changes hands. The restrictionist points to concentration: one donor above 291 million dollars in 2024. The restrictionist also points to a 2014 study by Gilens and Page, whose methods have been seriously contested. It found that policy follows economic elites, not average citizens.

- Dependence, not bribery

> The restrictionist: dependence on the largest funders corrupts without a bribe.

[check q2]

## The coordination-rules reformer

The coordination-rules reformer argues that the doctrine is sound if independence is real, and that enforcement has made it fictional. Independent spending is protected because it is not arranged with the candidate. The reformer points to the rules as written: a candidate's former campaign manager can run the supporting super PAC, and the candidate can headline its fundraisers, with no legal coordination. The remedy is tighter rules, and no constitutional amendment.

- Tighter rules, no amendment

> The reformer: independence is the premise, and the rules made it fiction.

[check q4]

## Settled and contested

Some of this is settled: the scale of the money, and that super PACs disclose their donors. What is contested is whether independent spending changes policy or chases it, and whether any restriction merely reroutes the money.

- Settled: the scale, the disclosure
- Contested: whether money changes policy

The lesson gives each camp its full case, the evidence that would change each one's mind, and a table that sets the three side by side.
