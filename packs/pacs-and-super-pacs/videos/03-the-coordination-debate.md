## A question from Texas

On March 20, 2024, the Federal Election Commission answered a question from a political committee in Texas. Could it pay for door-to-door canvassing in consultation with candidates and parties? The Commission said yes.

- Advisory Opinion 2024-01

[beat]

By that autumn, Donald Trump's campaign had handed much of its swing-state door-knocking to America PAC, Elon Musk's super PAC.

> An outside group ran a presidential ground game. Nothing about it was illegal.

## One word

Unlimited money may be spent on an election only on the premise that it is independent of the candidate. Under federal law, spending made in cooperation, consultation or concert with a candidate is treated as a contribution to that candidate.

- Coordinated spending counts as a contribution

So a coordinated 40 million dollar ad campaign becomes a 40 million dollar contribution, roughly 11,000 times the legal limit.

> Coordination turns independent spending into a contribution, far over the limit.

[check q1]

## Three prongs, all required

The Commission's test has three prongs: payment, content and conduct. A communication is coordinated only if it meets all three. Miss one, and it is independent.

- Payment
- Content
- Conduct

That is how the Texas answer worked. The rule applies only to public communications, and door-to-door contact involves no paid mass-media placement. So a candidate may sit with a super PAC and plan the ground game.

> Canvassing is not a public communication, so the coordination rule never attaches.

[check q3]

## Lawful as written

Most of the visible cooperation breaks no rule. Campaigns post strategy on public websites, a practice called redboxing, and super PACs act on what they find there. A campaign manager can leave in March and run the supporting super PAC by August, because the cooling-off window is 120 days.

- Redboxing: strategy posted in public
- Cooling-off window: 120 days

And when conduct arguably crosses the line, the Commission needs four of its six votes to act. Three to three deadlocks function as dismissals.

- 3 to 3 deadlocks act as dismissals

[check q4]

## What stays open

The reformer's point is that all of this is lawful, so the rules should be rewritten. The defender's rejoinder is that each rule reflects a deliberate choice to protect speech.

What remains open is whether the independence premise can be made true by better drafting, whether it was always a legal fiction, or whether its erosion is simply what robust political speech looks like.

- Fixable by better drafting?
- Always a legal fiction?
- What robust speech looks like?

The lesson walks each prong, catalogues the workarounds one by one, and sets each reform bill beside the objection it faces.
