## 72 laws, 2,441 rules

In 2025, the Congress of the United States, with its 535 members, enacted 72 public laws. The same year, federal agencies published 2,441 final rules. And 2025 was the quietest year of federal rulemaking since record-keeping began.

- Congress: 72 public laws
- Agencies: 2,441 final rules

> Who writes the law Americans live under? Mostly not Congress.

## A rule is law

A federal rule, once final, binds you exactly as a statute does. It carries the force of law, and courts enforce it. The emission limits on your car are rules. So are the disclosures in your mortgage.

It starts with a delegation. Congress passes a statute that creates an agency and hands it power. The statute states the goal and the boundaries, and the agency writes the binding specifics. Congress designed it this way: 535 generalists cannot set ladder standards.

- The statute: goal and boundaries
- The agency: the binding specifics

> A final rule carries the force of law.

## The gauntlet

A major rule runs a gauntlet, usually over two to four years. Under the Administrative Procedure Act of 1946, the agency publishes its proposal, takes comments from anyone, including you, and explains the final rule. Significant rules also pass a White House checkpoint, a small office called OIRA. And courts set a rule aside when the agency's reasoning is arbitrary or capricious.

- Notice and comment
- White House review: OIRA
- Courts: arbitrary or capricious

## The recall button

Congress kept a lever for itself. Under the Congressional Review Act of 1996, it can repeal a rule by joint resolution, with a simple majority and no filibuster. The catch: the resolution needs the president's signature. So the law lies dormant, then detonates at transitions, when a new Congress gets a fresh clock on every rule submitted in the final 60 legislative days of the old one. One rule was overturned in the law's first twenty years. In 2025, 22 resolutions were signed.

- Simple majority, no filibuster
- Lookback: the final 60 legislative days
- 2025: 22 resolutions signed
