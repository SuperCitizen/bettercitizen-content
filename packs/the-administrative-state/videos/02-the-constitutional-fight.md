## Black Monday

On the morning of May 27, 1935, the Supreme Court handed down two unanimous decisions within minutes of each other. In Schechter Poultry, it struck down a law because Congress had handed its legislative power to the executive with almost no instructions. In Humphrey's Executor, it held that President Roosevelt could not fire a Federal Trade Commissioner over policy disagreement.

- Schechter: a delegation struck down
- Humphrey's Executor: a commissioner protected

Read together, they drew a deal that governed for ninety years. Congress may delegate broadly, and may park some of that power in agencies the President does not fully command.

> Broad delegation, to agencies the President does not fully command.

## Can Congress give power away?

The test dates from 1928: a delegation stands so long as Congress supplies "an intelligible principle." Only two statutes have ever failed it, both in 1935. In June 2025, in FCC v. Consumers' Research, the Court upheld a delegation once more, six to three, holding that Congress had sufficiently guided the agency.

- Struck down: two statutes, both in 1935
- Consumers' Research, 2025: upheld 6–3

## Who says what a statute means?

For forty years, under a 1984 case called Chevron, courts had to accept any permissible reading an agency gave an ambiguous statute. In 2024, Loper Bright ended it: "Chevron is overruled." Courts must exercise their independent judgment. They may still give an agency's reading respect in proportion to its persuasiveness, and the earlier holdings that relied on Chevron stand.

- Chevron, 1984: accept the agency's reading
- Loper Bright, 2024: courts decide

> Ambiguity is no longer agency space. It is judicial space.

## Who controls the people?

On Humphrey's Executor, the independent agencies were built. In 2020, in Seila Law, the Court recast it as a narrow exception for multimember expert agencies. In 2025 President Trump removed commission members without cause, and interim orders have let every contested removal stand.

Humphrey's Executor has not been overruled. But Trump v. Slaughter, which asks whether it should be, was argued in December 2025, and was undecided as of mid-June 2026.

- Humphrey's Executor: narrowed, not overruled
- Trump v. Slaughter: argued, undecided

## What doctrine cannot answer

The deepest question is about what we want from government: whether the 1935 settlement was a betrayal of the Constitution's design, or its intelligent adaptation to industrial modernity.

> A betrayal of the Constitution's design, or its intelligent adaptation?
