## Five states to four

By the middle of July 1787, the Constitutional Convention in Philadelphia was close to falling apart. The delegates were stuck on a single question. Should a state's voice in the new legislature depend on how many people live there, or should every state count the same?

[beat]

On July 16 they voted on a way out. It passed, five states to four.

> A one-state margin put two legislatures inside one Congress.

## The Great Compromise

The populous states favored the Virginia Plan: two chambers, both apportioned by population. The small states favored the New Jersey Plan, under which every state had an equal vote.

Roger Sherman of Connecticut had been pushing a way through. Keep two chambers, but give them different foundations. History calls it the Great Compromise.

- House: seats follow population
- Senate: two per state

> Population in the House. One equal voice per state in the Senate.

## Two calendars

The chambers also run on different calendars. All 435 House seats are on the ballot at every election, every two years. Senators serve six years, staggered, so only about a third of the seats are up at a time.

- House: all 435 seats, every 2 years
- Senate: about a third at a time

A sudden swing in the national mood can change the whole House in one November. It has to last through at least two elections to reach most of the Senate.

> The House is built to respond. The Senate is built to endure.

## Two locks on one door

Picture a door with two locks, cut by two different locksmiths. A burglar who copies one key still cannot get in. A resident who has lost one key cannot either.

> Harder to do something bad, and harder to do anything at all.

## The price of an equal Senate

In the 2020 census, California counted about 39.5 million residents and Wyoming about 577,000. Both states get two senators.

- Roughly 68 Californians per Wyomingite

Whether that trade-off is worth it is a live argument. The case for says the Senate is where the states are represented as states, and that the equal vote was the price of the union. The case against, made by political scientist Robert Dahl, says it is among the Constitution's least democratic features.

- For: states represented as states
- Against: among the least democratic features
