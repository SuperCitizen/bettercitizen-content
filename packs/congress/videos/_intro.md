## Two bodies, one law

Every federal law has to win over two bodies. They are elected on different schedules, from different maps, by different arithmetic. Why would anyone build a legislature that way, and who actually runs it?

> Every federal law must pass two chambers, built on two different principles.

## What the pack covers

The first lesson asks why Congress has two chambers, starting with the Great Compromise of 1787.

- 1. Why two chambers?

The second asks who wins seats in Congress, and why incumbents rarely lose.

- 2. Who wins seats?

The third asks who runs Congress, from committee chairs to party leaders, and how members decide how to vote.

- 3. Who runs Congress?

## Where to start

Start with the first lesson. It begins in Philadelphia in July 1787, with a vote that passed five states to four.

- Start with lesson 1
