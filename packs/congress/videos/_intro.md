## Two bodies, one law

Every federal law has to win over two bodies. They are elected on different schedules, from different maps, by different arithmetic. Why would anyone build a legislature that way?

> Every federal law must pass two chambers, built on two different principles.

## What the pack covers

The pack opens with one lesson, and it asks why Congress has two chambers. It covers the Great Compromise of 1787, which reconciled a big-state plan and a small-state plan.

- 1. Why does Congress have two chambers?
- The Great Compromise

It compares the House and the Senate on size, terms and the powers each holds alone. And it weighs what a second chamber buys, and what it costs.

- What each chamber alone can do
- What the design buys and costs
