## Fifteen ballots

In January 2023, the House needed fifteen ballots to elect Kevin McCarthy as Speaker. No Speaker election had needed more than one since 1923. Nine months later, the House removed him. No Speaker had ever been removed before.

- 15 ballots, January 2023
- First Speaker ever removed

> Leaders keep their jobs only while enough members back them.

## Committees

In 2023 and 2024, members introduced 19,306 measures, and 274 became law. Committees decide which few move. The majority party holds most committee seats and every chair, and members get their seats through their party.

- 19,306 measures, 274 laws
- Every chair: majority party

## Leaders

The Constitution names only one House leader, the Speaker. A Speaker needs a majority of the votes cast for a person by name, so a "present" vote lowers the bar. In the Senate, the majority leader's power rests on a precedent from 1937: the chair recognizes the leader first.

- Speaker: majority of named votes
- Senate leader: recognized first

## Three answers

Should a member vote as the district wants, by her own judgment, or with her party? Political scientists call those the delegate, the trustee and the partisan. Most members blend them.

- Delegate, trustee, partisan

> A roll call records what a member did, not why.

[check q4]

## Party lines

In 2025, 85.3 percent of roll calls were party-unity votes, the highest share since the study began. Members of both parties still broke ranks on major votes. Scholars disagree about why party voting runs so high: shared views, or leaders who control the agenda.

- 2025 party-unity votes: 85.3%
- Shared views, or agenda control?
