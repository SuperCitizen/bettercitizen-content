## Sixteen percent

In October 2024, 16 percent of Americans approved of the way Congress was doing its job. That same year, 387 House members sought another term, and 371 of them won it.

- Congress approval: 16%
- House incumbents reelected: 95.9%

> People think poorly of Congress, and keep reelecting its members.

## What the number hides

The rate counts only members who chose to run. Many districts lean so far one way that any nominee of that party would win. And strong challengers tend to wait for an open seat.

- Who runs
- How the district leans
- Which challengers wait

Most House members first won by succeeding a retiring member of their own party. Fewer than one in five began by beating an incumbent.

> A reelection rate measures more than incumbency.

## The real election

Picture a district that votes 70 to 30 for one party. The November race there is settled before it starts. The election that can change who holds the seat is that party's primary, usually decided by a smaller, more committed crowd.

- Safe seat: decided in the primary

In 2024, primaries ended four House careers, two in each party.

- 2024 primary losses: two from each party

> In a safe seat, the primary is the real election.

## Who serves

Winning a seat takes money, connections, and usually a prior office. Women hold about 29 percent of the seats. And 96 percent of House members hold a bachelor's degree, against about 39 percent of adults.

- Women: 29% of seats
- Bachelor's degree: 96% vs. 39%

## Does it matter?

Whether Congress should look more like the country is a value question. Jane Mansbridge argued that shared experience changes what members notice and raise. Hanna Pitkin argued that voters should judge representatives by what they do, not by what they are.

- Mansbridge: a contingent yes
- Pitkin: judge what they do
