## Three minutes at the lectern

It is 6:58 on a Tuesday evening at city hall. A clerk hands you a paper card. When your turn comes you get a lectern, a microphone and a countdown timer: three minutes in most cities.

[beat]

No other level of American government is this easy to reach, and almost nobody reaches for it. But the lectern is not where most decisions are made.

> By the night of the final vote, positions are usually set.

## The right door

Before anything else, sort the problem. A pothole, a missed trash pickup or a broken streetlight is a service request. The fastest route is the department itself, usually through a 311 line.

- Service request: the department, via 311

Councils legislate. They do not dispatch crews. The council is the right door when the rule is the problem: the speed limit, the zoning category, the budget line.

- Policy request: the council and its committee

> A broken thing goes to the department. A broken rule goes to the council.

[check q1]

## One stage earlier

Most councils send their work through standing committees, such as Finance or Land Use. The committee hearing, not the full-council vote, is where text gets written and minds are open.

- Committee: where the text is written

The council meeting is where decisions are recorded. So show up one stage earlier than instinct suggests. Testify at the committee, and send your own written comments while the text is still being drafted.

- Council meeting: where the vote is recorded

The lesson lays out the whole path in five steps. The lectern sits fourth.

> Show up one stage earlier than instinct suggests.

## What three minutes can do

Public comment still does something. It creates a public record, and it signals that someone is watching. The lesson calls it the opening move, not the strategy.

- Comment builds the record

And the lesson promises less than you might hope. The evidence is that organized, early, persistent showing up changes outcomes. An ordinance takes months, and the realistic win is incremental: an amendment, a study, a relationship with a staffer.

- Organized, early, persistent

The lesson has the craft of a three-minute comment, the seats residents can hold above the lectern, and the heavy machinery: initiative, referendum and recall.
