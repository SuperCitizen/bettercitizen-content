## The city that changed its form

In January 2025, Portland, Oregon stopped being the last major American city run by a commission. In that form, each elected commissioner personally runs a slice of the bureaucracy: fire here, parks there. Voters had approved the overhaul in 2022, by about 57 percent.

[beat]

Nothing about that choice was dictated by the United States Constitution. City government is a menu, and most residents have never been told there was one.

> City government is a menu. Your city's pick decides who holds power.

## Where executive power lives

Two forms dominate. In a mayor-council city, voters elect a council and a mayor who is a genuine chief executive: hiring and firing department heads, drafting the budget, vetoing ordinances. New York, Chicago, Houston and Philadelphia work this way.

- Mayor-council: voters elect the executive

In a council-manager city, voters elect a council that sets policy and hires a professional city manager as chief executive. The mayor is often ceremonial. Phoenix, San Antonio, Dallas and San Jose work this way.

- Council-manager: the council hires the executive

So when snow removal fails in a council-manager city, who can residents vote out?

[pause]

Not the manager. Nobody elected the manager, and only the council can fire one. Voters' recourse is to replace council members until the council replaces the manager.

> Only the council can fire a city manager. Voters cannot.

## The state holds the leash

Cities appear nowhere in the Constitution. Under Dillon's Rule, municipalities are creatures of the state, with only the powers the legislature grants. The Supreme Court ratified that view in 1907.

- Dillon's Rule: cities are creatures of the state

Most states have softened the rule with home rule, which lets cities legislate on local affairs. But what the state grants, the state can preempt. In 2016 Birmingham, Alabama passed a local minimum wage. Days later, the state nullified it.

- Birmingham, 2016: nullified within days

> What the state grants, the state can preempt.

[check q2]
