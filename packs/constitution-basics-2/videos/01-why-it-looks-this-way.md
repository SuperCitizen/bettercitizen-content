## Behind shuttered windows

In May of 1787, delegates from twelve states shuttered the windows of the Pennsylvania State House and swore one another to secrecy. Their instructions were to revise the Articles of Confederation. By the middle of June they had decided to design a different government altogether.

[beat]

They did not agree with one another about nearly anything that mattered.

> The Constitution is a record of bargains among people who disagreed.

## Equal people, or equal states

The hardest fight was over seats in Congress. Large states such as Virginia wanted seats distributed by population. Small states such as Delaware wanted every state to have an equal vote.

- Large states: seats by population
- Small states: one equal vote each

The solution was to do both. In the House, seats would follow population. In the Senate, each state would have two members. On July 16, 1787, James Madison recorded the vote on equal votes in the Senate. Delaware and New Jersey voted aye. Virginia and Pennsylvania voted no.

> Equal votes in the Senate were the price the small states demanded.

## The word that is not there

Search the Constitution of 1787 for the word "slavery" and you will not find it. The delegates avoided the word, and then wrote three clauses that protected the institution.

- Three clauses, and never the word

One of them counted three-fifths of a state's enslaved population toward its seats in the House. Who wanted those people counted in full?

[pause]

Delegates from South Carolina did, because a larger count meant more seats for states that gave those same people no vote and no rights. Several northern delegates argued that they should not be counted at all.

- Slaveholding states wanted a full count
- Three-fifths: where the two sides settled

## One fact, two conclusions

Nine states had to approve the plan, and the argument was only beginning. A writer called Brutus argued that a country this large could not stay free, because officials that far away escape the people's control. Madison answered that size is the remedy: in a large republic so many interests compete that none can easily dominate the others.

- Brutus: distance is the danger
- Madison: size is the remedy

> Both arguments are still made, a sign that neither was simply wrong.

## What stays open

Historians still disagree about whether the bargains over slavery were the price of a union or a moral failure the delegates could have refused.

- The price of union, or a moral failure
