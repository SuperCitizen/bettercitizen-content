## A document full of bargains

The Constitution was not handed down by people who agreed with one another. It was written in 1787 by delegates who disagreed about nearly everything that mattered. Many of its strangest features mark the places where a bargain was struck.

> Why does it look this way, and why is it so hard to change?

## Two lessons

The first lesson asks why the Constitution looks the way it does. It covers the bargain that gave every state two senators, and the three clauses that protected slavery. It ends with two writers, Brutus and James Madison, arguing over whether so large a country could stay free.

- 1. Why it looks the way it does

The second asks why the Constitution is so hard to change. It covers the rule for amendments and the dispute over the Equal Rights Amendment. Then Madison and Thomas Jefferson disagree about whether a constitution should be hard to change.

- 2. Why it is so hard to change
