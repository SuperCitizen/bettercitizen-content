## Rights against whom?

In 1833 a Baltimore wharf owner sued his city under the Fifth Amendment. The Supreme Court told him the Bill of Rights did not apply to his case at all. For most of American history, the first ten amendments restrained only the federal government.

> What a right says is one question. Whom it binds is another.

## Three lessons

The first lesson shows what the ten amendments protect, and why they were forced into the Constitution in 1791.

- 1. What the Bill of Rights actually protects

The second follows how those rights came to bind the states, one clause at a time, through the Fourteenth Amendment.

- 2. Incorporation

The third gives the quiet amendments their due: the Third, Seventh, Ninth and Tenth, which rarely reach a courtroom yet still shape constitutional argument.

- 3. The quiet amendments
