## The loser who won

Benjamin Gitlow lost his case. A socialist organizer from the Bronx, he was convicted under New York's criminal anarchy law for publishing a pamphlet. In June 1925 the Supreme Court upheld the conviction, seven to two.

- Gitlow v. New York, 1925

But the majority assumed, almost in passing, that freedom of speech and press are among the liberties the Fourteenth Amendment protects from the states.

> With one sentence, the Court began demolishing a wall that had stood since 1833.

## The amendment built for the job

The wall was Barron v. Baltimore: the first ten amendments restrain only federal action. The Fourteenth Amendment, in 1868, was written to breach it. Introducing it, Senator Jacob Howard said its privileges and immunities included the personal rights secured by the first eight amendments.

- 1868: "No State shall…"

Then came the Slaughter-House Cases of 1873. The Court held that the privileges or immunities clause protects only a short list of rights of national citizenship. The natural home for applying the Bill of Rights to the states was a dead letter within five years.

- 1873: Slaughter-House narrows the clause

## One clause at a time

So the work went through a different clause: due process. Rights crossed one at a time, in a doctrine called selective incorporation. Since Duncan v. Louisiana in 1968, the Court asks whether a guarantee is "fundamental to the American scheme of justice." Once a clause crosses, it applies to the states with exactly the same force as against the federal government.

- Selective incorporation
- Test: fundamental to the American scheme
- Same force against the states

> The Court absorbs rights clause by clause, each at full federal force.

[check q2-selective-test]

## A right with a date

So each right reached the states in its own year. Appointed counsel crossed with Gideon v. Wainwright in 1963, and jury trial with Duncan in 1968. The Second Amendment crossed in 2010, and the excessive-fines clause in 2019.

- Gideon, 1963: appointed counsel
- McDonald, 2010: Second Amendment
- Timbs, 2019: excessive fines

[check q3-timing-transfer]

## Not quite finished

As of mid-2026, two guarantees remain unincorporated: the Fifth Amendment's grand-jury clause and the Seventh Amendment's civil-jury right. The route is disputed too. In 2010 Justice Thomas argued for reviving the privileges or immunities clause. The Court has so far declined.

- Still outside: grand jury, civil jury
- Disputed: which clause should carry it

> An amendment ratified in 1868 was still producing holdings in 2020.
