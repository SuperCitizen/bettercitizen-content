## A President told no

In 1952, President Harry Truman ordered the nation's steel mills seized. The Supreme Court told him to give them back, and he did.

[beat]

Why could a court say that to a President, and why did he comply? The answer starts with how the Constitution divides federal power.

> Federal power is split three ways, and each branch can stop the others.

## Two lessons

The first lesson maps the architecture: what Congress, the President and the courts each do, what each cannot do, and Madison's case for separating them.

- 1. How power is divided

The second inventories the enforcement machinery of checks and balances, from the veto and the purse to judicial review, through the cases where the Supreme Court had to referee.

- 2. Checks and balances

Neither lesson treats the subject as finished. Whether the checks still work as Madison predicted is a live argument, and the pack says so.
