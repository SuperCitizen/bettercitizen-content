## A letter from a prison cell

Clarence Earl Gideon was arrested in Florida and charged with breaking into a building. He had no money. When his trial began, he asked the judge to give him a lawyer.

The judge said no. So Gideon had to be his own lawyer. He lost, and he was sent to prison for five years.

- Clarence Earl Gideon, Florida
- No money, and no lawyer

## All nine justices

From his cell, Gideon wrote a letter by hand to the Supreme Court. He said the Constitution promised him a lawyer. In 1963, all nine justices agreed with him.

Since then, any person accused of a serious crime who cannot pay for a lawyer must be given one. Gideon got a new trial, with a lawyer this time. The jury found him not guilty.

- 1963: all nine justices agree

> A person accused of a serious crime must be given a lawyer.

## Half of the list

Gideon's promise came from the Sixth Amendment. Half of the Bill of Rights, the Fourth through the Eighth Amendments, is about how the government must treat people it suspects of a crime.

Why protect them? Because being accused is not the same as being guilty. Anyone can be accused, including someone who did nothing wrong.

The Fourth Amendment is one example. The police need a good reason, and often a judge's permission, to search you or your home.

- Fourth: a good reason to search
- Sixth: a trial, a jury, a lawyer

> Being accused is not the same as being guilty.

## A surprise

When the Bill of Rights was new, it limited only the national government. A state could ignore most of it.

That changed after the Civil War. In 1868 the Fourteenth Amendment was added. Over the next hundred years and more, the Supreme Court used its words to make the states follow almost all of the Bill of Rights. Gideon's case was one step. Florida, a state, had to obey the Sixth Amendment.

- At first: only the national government
- 1868: the Fourteenth Amendment

> At first, the Bill of Rights did not limit the states.

[check q4]

## Still being argued

People still argue about what the words mean. What makes a search "unreasonable"? What makes a punishment "cruel"? Each time a new question comes up, a court has to decide.

- The words are still argued over
