## The man who would not sign

On September 17, 1787, the men who had written the Constitution lined up to sign it. George Mason of Virginia would not pick up the pen.

[beat]

Mason had worked on the plan all summer. He agreed with much of it. But he had found something missing.

- September 17, 1787
- George Mason would not sign

## What was missing

The Constitution said a great deal about what the new government could do. It said almost nothing about what the government could not do to ordinary people.

Mason wanted a written list of rights. A right is a freedom that belongs to you, and that the government has to respect. A list like that is called a bill of rights.

- A right: a freedom the government must respect
- A bill of rights: a written list

> Mason would not sign a Constitution that had no list of rights.

[check q1]

## Signs that face the government

Here is a way to picture it. Imagine a row of signs that say Keep Out. Every sign faces the government. Behind the signs are the freedoms that belong to the people.

A bill of rights does not tell citizens what to do. It tells the government where it has to stop.

> A bill of rights tells the government where it has to stop.

## Was a list needed?

Not everyone thought so. James Madison had done more than anyone to design the Constitution. At first he said a list was not needed. The government could only use the powers the Constitution gave it, he argued.

- Madison at first: no list needed

The other side had a simple answer. If the government was never going to take those freedoms, what was the harm in saying so in writing?

[pause]

The argument nearly sank the Constitution. Several states approved it only after they were promised a list of rights.

- Several states wanted a promise first

## Ten amendments

Madison kept the promise himself. On June 8, 1789, in the very first Congress, he proposed a list of amendments. An amendment is a change added to the Constitution.

The states approved ten of them. On December 15, 1791, those ten became the Bill of Rights.

- June 8, 1789: Madison proposes amendments
- December 15, 1791: ten are approved

> The Bill of Rights is the first ten amendments to the Constitution.
