## Smaller than a tank of gas

In the 2024 cycle, ActBlue, the Democratic side's online donation platform, processed about 3.8 billion dollars. Its Republican counterpart, WinRed, processed about 1.7 billion. Most of that arrived in increments smaller than a tank of gas, from millions of ordinary people, on phones.

- ActBlue: about $3.8 billion
- WinRed: about $1.7 billion

If you have ever chipped in 25 dollars, you are a participant in this system.

> The remaining question is whether you participate deliberately.

## The 200 dollar line

Start with the channel, because the same gift behaves differently depending on where it lands. Then know the disclosure trade. Once your giving to any one committee passes 200 dollars in a cycle, your name, address, employer and occupation become a permanently public federal record.

- Over $200 in a cycle: public
- Name, address, employer, occupation

A cycle runs two years. Recurring gifts are how most donors cross the line without noticing.

> Neither outcome is wrong. But it should be chosen, not discovered.

## Who is actually asking?

Fundraising emails routinely come from PACs with candidate-sounding names, which may share only a vendor with the candidate. The fine print at the bottom, Paid for by, names the real committee. Look it up before you assume.

- Read the "Paid for by" line

Then check the record, not the ads: how they voted, and who already funds them.

- The record, not the ads

## Built to hurry you

Online fundraising is engineered: countdown clocks, claims of matches that no law verifies, and boxes checked in advance that turn one gift into monthly withdrawals. None of this is illegal. All of it is designed to get the gift before reflection arrives.

- Uncheck the recurring box

## The small-donor paradox

Small gifts look like the obvious cure for big money. The research complicates that. One argument holds that the donors most motivated to give are ideological purists, so donor-driven money favors the most polarizing candidates. Another finds that most giving is participation: people give the way they cheer. That tension is unresolved.

- Purists, or participation?

The lesson names the tools for each step, and leaves you one habit.

> Channel, disclosure, record: in that order, before the money moves.
