## Two numbers, both legal

In the 2024 election cycle, Elon Musk put more than 290 million dollars into electing his preferred candidates. The legal ceiling on what he could hand Donald Trump's own campaign committee was 6,600 dollars, for the primary and general combined.

- To the campaign itself: $6,600
- In all: more than $290 million

Both facts are true at once, and neither involved breaking any law.

> The distance between those two numbers is what every money argument is about.

## Three questions

For any dollar moving through politics, ask three things. Is it capped? Is it disclosed? And can it be coordinated with the candidate?

- Is it capped?
- Is it disclosed?
- Can it be coordinated?

There are five legally distinct channels a political dollar can flow through. They are the five ways those three questions get answered.

## Capped, and disclosed

Three channels are capped. In the current cycle, an individual can give a candidate's committee 3,500 dollars per election, and the primary and the general count separately. A national party can take 44,300 dollars a year. A traditional PAC can take 5,000 dollars a year. All three are disclosed.

- Candidate: $3,500 per election
- National party: $44,300 a year
- Traditional PAC: $5,000 a year

Corporations have been barred from contributing since 1907. So a corporate PAC is funded by voluntary contributions from employees, not by the corporate treasury.

- Corporate PAC: employee money, not treasury

## Unlimited, and independent

Two channels have no cap. A Super PAC can accept unlimited money from individuals, corporations and unions. Its donors are disclosed, and it must not coordinate with the candidate. Musk's money traveled almost entirely through this channel.

- Super PAC: unlimited, disclosed

A 501(c)(4) nonprofit can also take unlimited money, and its donors are never publicly disclosed. The public sees the spending. It cannot see who paid. This is the channel called dark money.

- 501(c)(4): unlimited, donors hidden

> Money a candidate controls is capped. Money spent independently is unlimited.

## Is it a lot?

The 2024 federal elections cost about 15.9 billion dollars. One famous article argued the puzzle is why there is so little: most political money behaves like consumption, not investment. The strongest reply is that the harm is not votes purchased, but whom candidates spend their time courting.

- 2024 federal elections: about $15.9 billion
