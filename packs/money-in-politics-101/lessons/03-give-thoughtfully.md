# Giving thoughtfully — research first, decide for yourself

You've now seen the four channels political money flows through and the law that drew the lines. The last piece of money-in-politics literacy is personal: when *you* give, how do you do it in a way that matches your values and your understanding?

This is not a step to skip. Deciding where your money goes is one of the most direct forms of civic participation there is. SuperCitizen won't tell you whom to support, and it won't move your money for you. What it can do is show you how to look before you give.

## 1. Know which channel you're giving into

The same \$100 behaves very differently depending on where it lands:

- To a **candidate's campaign committee** — capped per election, and your name, address, and employer are disclosed above \$200 aggregate.
- To a **Super PAC** — unlimited, independent of the candidate, donors itemized at the FEC.
- To a **501(c)(4)** — unlimited and **not** publicly disclosed ("dark money").

Ask yourself: what do I actually want this money to *do* — and am I comfortable with the disclosure trade-off of the channel I'm using?

## 2. Follow the money before you add to it

Before giving, see who already funds the candidate or group:

- **OpenSecrets** maps disclosed donors and industries behind a candidate, party, or PAC.
- **FEC data** is the official record — use it to verify a claim rather than taking an ad's word for it.

If the funding picture doesn't match the story a candidate tells about themselves, that's worth knowing before you give.

## 3. Check the record, not the ads

- **Vote Smart** collects votes, public statements, and interest-group ratings — across the spectrum.
- **Ballotpedia** gives a neutral overview of the race or ballot measure.

The goal isn't to reach a particular conclusion. It's to give from the record instead of from a 30-second spot.

## 4. Decide for yourself

SuperCitizen is nonpartisan by design. We don't host a "give to this candidate" button, we don't rank or recommend recipients, and we take no cut of anything. Political contributions are yours to make — directly, through the recipient or a registered platform you choose, informed by what you found above.

If you want to support nonpartisan civic education *itself*, you can support SuperCitizen at [supercitizen.org](https://supercitizen.org) — the home of our nonprofit mission. That keeps this work free and independent.

## Why this matters

Money is one of the loudest voices in politics. Giving with clear eyes — knowing the channel, the disclosure, and the record — turns a reflex into a considered civic act. That's the whole point of learning how the system works: not to opt out of it, but to participate in it deliberately.
