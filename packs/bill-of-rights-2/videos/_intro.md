## Two questions about ten amendments

Most of the government a person actually meets is state or local: the police officer, the public school, the county courthouse. When the Bill of Rights was new, it had nothing to say about any of them.

[beat]

So two questions come before any single right. Whom do the first ten amendments restrain? And how did they come to restrain the states as well as Washington?

> Whom does the Bill of Rights restrain, and since when?

## Two lessons

The first lesson asks who the Bill of Rights protects you from. It starts with Federalist 84, where Hamilton argued that no such list was needed, and ends with the tests that mark the limits of free speech.

- 1. Who it protects you from

The second follows the Bill of Rights across the line to the states, one case at a time, from Barron v. Baltimore to Gideon and McDonald.

- 2. How it reached the states

## Where to start

Start with the first lesson, Who does the Bill of Rights protect you from? It opens with two rulings from 1969, for two speakers with nothing else in common.
