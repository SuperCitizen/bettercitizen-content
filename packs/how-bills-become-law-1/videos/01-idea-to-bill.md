## A walk of 1,400 miles

In 2016, a retired teacher named Opal Lee laced up her shoes in Fort Worth, Texas. She was 89 years old. She set out to walk toward Washington, D.C., about 1,400 miles away.

- Opal Lee, age 89
- Toward Washington: about 1,400 miles

She wanted Juneteenth to be a holiday for the whole country. But a walk cannot make a holiday. Neither can a petition, even one with more than a million names on it.

> Only Congress can make a law.

## Anyone can have the idea

Ideas for laws come from everywhere. They come from voters and teachers, from doctors and farmers, and from the President. Anyone at all can write an idea down and send it to Congress.

- Anyone can send an idea

Everyone who lives in a state has three people there to send it to: one member of the House of Representatives and two senators.

- 1 representative, 2 senators

That is the easy part. An idea on paper is still only an idea.

## Only a member can introduce it

Here is the catch. Only a member of Congress can turn an idea into a bill. A bill is a written plan for a new law.

- Bill: a written plan for a new law

The member who brings it to Congress is called its sponsor. Bringing it is called introducing the bill.

- Sponsor: the member who introduces it

Say your class writes down a great idea for a law about school lunches. It is not a bill yet. It becomes a bill only when a member of the House or the Senate introduces it.

> Only a member of Congress can introduce a bill.

## The box and the number

In the House, a member introduces a bill by dropping it into a box called the hopper. Once the bill is in the box, it gets a number. Bills that start in the House begin with the letters H and R. Bills that start in the Senate begin with the letter S.

- The hopper: the House's box for bills
- H.R. means a House bill
- S. means a Senate bill

## Three days, after five years

Ms. Lee's idea got its number in 2021, when a senator introduced it. The Senate passed the bill on June 15. The House passed it the next day. On June 17, the President signed it, and Juneteenth became a national holiday.

- Signed June 17, 2021

Do not be fooled by those three days. Five years had gone by since her walk began, and most bills never get a week like that one.
