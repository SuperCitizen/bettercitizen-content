## Nineteen here, twenty-one there

In the early 1980s, the states did not agree about alcohol. In South Dakota, a person who was nineteen could buy a weak kind of beer. Other states made people wait until they were twenty-one.

- South Dakota: 19
- Other states: 21

Young people were driving to states with a lower age. They drank there, and then they drove home.

Congress wanted the age to be twenty-one everywhere. But choosing that age had always been a job for the states.

## An offer instead of an order

Congress found another way. In 1984 it passed a law about the money it gives the states to build highways. Any state that let people under twenty-one buy alcohol would lose part of that money.

- 1984: a law about highway money

## South Dakota goes to court

South Dakota took the national government to court. The state argued that Congress was using money to take over a job that belonged to the states.

In 1987 the Supreme Court ruled against South Dakota, by a vote of seven to two. The Court said the state was still free to keep its own rule. If it did, it would lose only five percent of that highway money.

- 1987: the Court rules 7 to 2
- The cost of saying no: 5%

> Congress had made an offer. It had not given an order.

## Can an offer go too far?

The Court has also said that an offer can turn into a threat. In 2012 it looked at a law about a health program for people with little money. A state that refused to make the program bigger could lose all of its money for the program.

The Court said that was no real choice. The chief justice called it "a gun to the head."

> An offer must leave a state a way to say no.

## One rule, or many?

Americans have made two arguments since 1787. One is to let each state decide. People in different places want different things, and states can test new ideas.

- Let each state decide

The other is to make one rule for all. Your rights should not change at a state line, and some problems cross from one state into the next.

- Make one rule for all

People often change sides, depending on which rule they like.
