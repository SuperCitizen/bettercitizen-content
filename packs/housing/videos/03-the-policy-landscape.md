## A town of under 40,000

Grants Pass is a southern Oregon timber town of under 40,000 people. Its city council barred sleeping in public with so much as a blanket, enforced with fines of 295 dollars. The town had no general homeless shelter to send people to. In June 2024, the Supreme Court used that little city to answer a question every mayor in America was asking.

- Grants Pass, Oregon: $295 fines
- No general homeless shelter

It is only one front. Since 2018, a dozen-odd states have overridden local zoning.

## Who can still say no?

California ran the cleanest design experiment in the country. Its law called SB 9 allowed two units, plus lot splits, on most single-family parcels. By the end of 2023, only 266 projects had been permitted or completed statewide. Its laws on accessory dwelling units, called ADUs, made them approvable by right. Annual ADU permits grew from 1,336 in 2016 to 26,924 in 2023.

- SB 9: 266 projects by end of 2023
- ADUs: 26,924 permits in 2023

The difference is process. ADU approval is ministerial: no hearing, no discretion, capped fees. SB 9 left cities room to smother projects in standards and fees.

- By right: no hearing, capped fees

> Passing a law is not producing housing.

## What the Court held

In the Grants Pass case, the Court ruled 6 to 3 that enforcing public-camping ordinances against people with nowhere else to sleep is not cruel and unusual punishment. The ruling removed a federal constitutional barrier. Whether enforcement without housing alternatives reduces homelessness or shuffles it is the question the opinion left to politics.

- Grants Pass v. Johnson: 6 to 3

> The ruling removed a constitutional barrier. It mandated nothing.

## The pivot on Housing First

Politics answered quickly. In July 2025, an executive order directed agencies to end federal support for Housing First, the model that provides permanent housing without sobriety or treatment preconditions.

Studies consistently show Housing First outperforms treatment-first models at getting and keeping people housed. Evidence that it by itself resolves addiction or psychiatric illness, or reliably saves public money, is much weaker. That is the gap both camps now argue from.

- Stronger: getting and keeping people housed
- Weaker: addiction, illness, public cost

## What a position requires

The lesson closes with what a defensible position requires. A view on whether the binding constraint is supply, demand-side assistance, or tenant power. A view on which level of government should decide. A view on the people unsheltered tonight. And an honest account of what evidence would change your mind.

- Supply, assistance, or tenant power
- Which level of government decides
- What would change your mind
