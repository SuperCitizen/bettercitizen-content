## Would it end racism?

At a Nevada rally in February 2016, Hillary Clinton put a question to her supporters. If we broke up the big banks tomorrow, would that end racism? Would it end sexism? The crowd shouted no.

- Clinton, Nevada, February 2016

The jab was aimed at Bernie Sanders. The argument is far older than either candidate.

> Which injustice organizes the others, and which programs actually deliver?

## The New Deal liberal stream

Progressivism has three major streams. The New Deal liberal stream is committed to economic security as the foundation of democratic citizenship. It argues that universal programs are politically more durable than targeted ones, and that they deliver disproportionate benefits to historically excluded groups anyway.

- Core: economic security

> The New Deal liberal case: universal programs last, and still reach the excluded.

## The identity-justice stream

The identity-justice stream is committed to addressing directly the exclusion of Black, Indigenous, women, LGBTQ and other marginalized groups. It argues from the New Deal's own record: Social Security at first excluded agricultural and domestic workers, who were overwhelmingly Black. A program can be called universal and still exclude by design.

- Core: exclusions addressed directly

> The identity-justice case: some harms cannot be reached by class-wide programs.

[check q2]

## The civic-republican stream

The civic-republican stream is committed to democratic citizenship as a shared activity, not just a bundle of individual rights. It argues that the meritocratic ideal itself corrodes solidarity: it tells winners they earned everything, and losers they deserve their lot.

- Core: citizenship as shared activity

> The civic-republican case: redistribution alone does not build solidarity.

[check q3]

## Universal or targeted

On much, the streams differ in framing more than in policy. All three strongly support universal healthcare and a universal child allowance. They split on reparations and on affirmative action.

- Agree: healthcare, child allowance
- Split: reparations, affirmative action

> The family's deepest divide: universal programs, or targeted ones.
