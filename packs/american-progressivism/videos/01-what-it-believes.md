## A word bigger than a party

In 1912, a former Republican president ran against his own party's sitting incumbent, on the ticket of the brand-new Progressive Party. Theodore Roosevelt took 27 percent of the popular vote.

- 1912: Roosevelt takes 27%

He lost to Woodrow Wilson, a Democrat who claimed the progressive mantle just as insistently. Two men from two parties spent a campaign fighting over the same word.

> Progressivism is a family of positions, not a party platform.

## What the family shares

Most progressives share a core. Government is a tool of public purpose: one of the instruments through which a democratic public pursues its values. Expert judgment and evidence are central to good governance. Widening the circle of rights is central to the project. And equality has to be more than a rule on paper. Equal opportunity requires positive action.

- Active government
- Expertise and evidence
- Inclusion
- Substantive equality

> Equal treatment under law is necessary, but not sufficient.

## Built in waves

The commitments were forged in waves, and each wave left institutions behind. The Progressive Era left regulatory agencies and three constitutional amendments. The New Deal left Social Security, the National Labor Relations Board and securities regulation. The Great Society added the Civil Rights Act in 1964, and Medicare and Medicaid in 1965.

- Progressive Era: 1890–1920
- New Deal: Social Security
- Great Society: Medicare and Medicaid

## Wisdom, or power?

Progressives hold that inherited authority is presumptively suspect. Their argument is that many inherited institutions were the product of accumulated power, not accumulated wisdom: slavery, gender exclusion, racial caste. An old arrangement may be defensible on its merits. Its age alone earns it nothing.

- Inherited hierarchy: presumptively suspect

> The progressive reply to tradition: longevity is evidence of survival, not of wisdom.

Conservatives contest each of these claims. The disagreement is about what counts as freedom, and about how specific institutions actually operate.

- Conservatives contest each claim

## Three readings of the inheritance

Inside the family, the inheritance is contested. New Deal liberals emphasize economic security, labor and the regulatory state. Identity-justice progressives emphasize that the New Deal coalition rested on compromises that excluded Black Americans, women and LGBTQ individuals from much of its benefits. Civic-republican progressives emphasize citizenship, civic participation and deliberation.

- New Deal liberals: economic security
- Identity-justice: end the exclusions
- Civic-republican: shared citizenship
