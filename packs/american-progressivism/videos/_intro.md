## A word two parties fought over

In 1912, a Republican former president and a Democrat spent a campaign fighting over one word: progressive. This pack sets out what that tradition believes, in language its adherents would recognize.

> Progressivism is a family of positions, not a single creed.

## Three lessons

The first lesson gives the commitments most progressives share, the thinkers behind them, and the waves that built the tradition, from the Progressive Era through the New Deal to the present.

- 1. What American progressivism actually believes

The second takes the three streams of today: New Deal liberal, identity-justice and civic-republican. Each one makes its strongest case against the other two, and none is declared the winner.

- 2. Internal disagreements within the tradition

The third maps the borders with conservatism, libertarianism, socialism and communitarianism.

- 3. How progressivism relates to other traditions

## Where to start

Start with the first lesson, What American progressivism actually believes. It opens with the election of 1912.
