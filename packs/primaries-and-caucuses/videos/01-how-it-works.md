## The primary that did not count

On January 23, 2024, New Hampshire held a presidential primary that the Democratic National Committee had declared "meaningless." The party had ordered the state to vote later. State law says New Hampshire votes first. The state obeyed its law.

[beat]

President Biden, following party rules, was not on the ballot. He won anyway, with more than 63 percent as a write-in.

- Biden, by write-in: more than 63%

## Two sovereigns

That collision shows who runs an American nomination. Political parties, which are formally private associations, write the rules for who becomes a nominee. State governments administer most of the voting. No federal law dictates how it is done.

- Parties: the rules
- States: most of the voting

> A nomination is run by two sovereigns at once.

The party's enforcement tool is delegate seating. New Hampshire's delegates were stripped, then quietly seated at the convention months later.

## A primary or a caucus

A primary is an election run by the state, with a secret ballot. A caucus is a meeting run by the party. In the classic Democratic form, neighbors gather and sort themselves into groups by candidate. In most precincts, a group below 15 percent of the room is not viable. Its members get one chance to realign: join a viable group, combine with another small group, or go home.

- Primary: state-run, secret ballot
- Caucus: party-run, in public
- Viability: 15% in most precincts

## Delegates, not votes

A nomination is won in delegates, and the two parties count differently. Democrats divide delegates in proportion to the vote, with a 15 percent threshold. A candidate at 12 percent everywhere wins no delegates at all. Republicans let states award every delegate to the winner from March 15. There, a 40 percent win can take 100 percent of the delegates.

- Democrats: proportional, 15% threshold
- Republicans: winner-take-all allowed from March 15

> Democratic races grind on. Republican races snap shut.

## Over before most have voted

The contests come in sequence, and states race to the front. In 2024, both nominations were clinched on the same night, March 12, before roughly half the country's primaries.

- Both clinched: March 12, 2024
