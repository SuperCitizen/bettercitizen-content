## The closed shutters

In May of 1787, a group of men walked into the State House in Philadelphia. A hot summer was coming, but they closed the shutters on the windows anyway. They did not want anyone outside to hear them.

They had been sent to fix the rules of their country. Before the summer was over, they had decided to write new ones.

> They were sent to fix the old rules. They wrote new ones.

## Rules for a government

A constitution is the set of rules for a government. It says who is in charge of what, and what the government is not allowed to do. Think of the rulebook for a sport.

- Constitution: the rules for a government

The United States already had rules in 1787. They were called the Articles of Confederation, and they made the national government very weak. Congress could ask the states for money, but it could not make them pay. Many leaders feared the United States would not last.

- The first rules: too weak

## The biggest fight

The men in that room were called delegates, which means people sent to speak for others. Their biggest fight was about Congress. States with many people wanted more votes. The small states wanted every state to have the same number.

In the end they made a compromise, which is a deal where each side gives up something. Congress would have two parts. In the House of Representatives, a state with more people gets more members. In the Senate, every state gets two members, no matter its size.

- House: more people, more members
- Senate: two for every state

> A compromise is a deal where each side gives up something.

## What the deals protected

The Constitution of 1787 did not end slavery. The delegates made deals that protected it. One deal counted three out of every five enslaved people when deciding how many House members a state would get. That gave more power to the states with slavery, even though the enslaved people could not vote.

Slavery did not end until 1865.

- 1787: slavery protected
- 1865: slavery ended

> The Constitution of 1787 did not end slavery. It protected it.

## Then the states had to agree

The Constitution was ready on September 17, 1787. It still was not the law, because nine of the thirteen states had to approve it first.

- Nine of thirteen states had to approve

The lesson tells how that approval was won, why one small state said yes to the compromise, and how fourteen years led from the first plan to the Bill of Rights.
