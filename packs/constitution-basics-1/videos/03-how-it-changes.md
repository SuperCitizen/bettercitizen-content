## A paper that got a C

In 1982, a college student in Texas named Gregory Watson was looking for a topic for a paper. He came across a change to the Constitution that Congress had proposed back in 1789. Not enough states had ever approved it. But nothing in it said that time had run out.

He wrote that the states could still approve it. His teacher gave him a C.

- 1982: Gregory Watson's paper

## Ten years of letters

He did not give up. He spent the next ten years writing letters to lawmakers in state after state. In 1992, the idea he had found became part of the Constitution.

A change to the Constitution is called an amendment. There are 27 amendments, and Watson's is the last one.

- Amendment: a change to the Constitution
- 27 amendments

> It can still be done, even by one person with a stack of letters.

## Two steps, and both are hard

First, an amendment has to be proposed. Two-thirds of the House and two-thirds of the Senate must vote for it.

- Step 1: 2/3 of House and Senate

Second, it has to be ratified, which means approved by the states. Three-fourths of the states must say yes. Today that means 38 of the 50 states. The President has no part in it.

- Step 2: 38 of 50 states

Members of Congress have suggested about 12,000 amendments since 1789. Only 27 have made it all the way.

> One state can be the difference between an idea and an amendment.

[check q2]

## Should it be this hard?

Two of the men who built the country disagreed about that.

James Madison thought the Constitution should be changed rarely. He worried that if people rewrote the rules all the time, they would stop respecting them. A government needs the trust that only comes with time, he wrote.

- Madison: change the rules rarely

Thomas Jefferson thought each new group of Americans should be free to fix what no longer worked. He wrote that old rules kept forever would be like a coat that fitted a man when he was a boy.

- Jefferson: fix what no longer works

Both of them had a point. Article Five sits in between.

> Change is allowed, but only when most of the country agrees.
