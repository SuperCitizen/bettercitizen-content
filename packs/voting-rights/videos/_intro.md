## Never settled once

In 1965, 6.7 percent of voting-age Black citizens in Mississippi were registered to vote. The Constitution had barred denying the vote on account of race ninety-five years earlier. Who gets to vote in America has never been settled once.

> The vote has expanded, contracted, and expanded again, for 250 years.

## Three lessons

The first lesson traces the franchise from property qualifications through the 15th, 19th, 24th and 26th Amendments.

- 1. The long expansion

The second takes apart the Voting Rights Act of 1965, and what remains of it after Shelby County, Brnovich and Milligan.

- 2. The Voting Rights Act

The third gives the strongest case for each position in the fights over registration, list maintenance, felony disenfranchisement and proof of citizenship.

- 3. The live debates

The mechanics and the case law are verifiable. What counts as a burden on the vote, and who should bear the cost of error, is where the argument continues.
