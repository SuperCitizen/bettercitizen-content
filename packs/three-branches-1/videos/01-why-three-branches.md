## One kid makes all the rules

Picture a kickball game at recess. One kid makes up all the rules. The same kid decides who broke them, and hands out the punishments too.

[beat]

Then the rule-maker's own team starts to lose. The rules change, and the calls all go one way. Nobody can stop it, because one person holds all the power.

> When one person holds all the power, nobody can stop the cheating.

## Three jobs, three branches

The people who wrote the United States Constitution worried about this same problem. They saw that every government does three main jobs. It makes laws. It carries out those laws. And it decides what the laws mean when people disagree.

The Constitution gives each job to a different group. Congress makes the laws, and it is called the legislative branch. The President carries out the laws, and that is the executive branch. The courts decide what the laws mean, and they are the judicial branch.

- Congress: makes the laws
- The President: carries out the laws
- The courts: decide what laws mean

Splitting up the jobs this way is called the separation of powers. It means that no single branch gets to do all three.

> Separation of powers: no branch does all three jobs.

## If men were angels

One of the writers was James Madison. In 1788 he wrote a line that people still quote today. "If men were angels, no government would be necessary."

- James Madison, 1788

Madison meant that people are not perfect. Even a good leader can be tempted to grab more power. So the plan does not count on leaders being good. It counts on power being split up among them.

> The plan does not count on leaders being good.

[check q4]

Madison also gave a warning. Putting all three powers in the same hands, he wrote, is "the very definition of tyranny." Tyranny means cruel and unfair rule by someone with too much power.

- Tyranny: unfair rule by too much power

## Where the kickball picture breaks down

A kickball game is not a country. On the playground, three kids could do their jobs without ever talking to each other. The real branches cannot work that way. They have to work together, and each one can block the others in certain ways.

- The branches work together
- Each one can block the others

The lesson tells what came first: a king, and then a first plan that was too weak. It also shows which part of the Constitution sets up each branch. The next two lessons ask who does what, and what happens when the branches disagree.
