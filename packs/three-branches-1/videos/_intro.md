## The kid with all the power

Picture a kickball game where one kid makes up all the rules. The same kid decides who broke them, and hands out the punishments too. What happens when that kid's own team starts to lose?

The people who wrote the Constitution worried about this same problem. Their answer was to split the government's power into three parts, which we call the three branches.

> The Constitution splits the government's power into three branches.

## Three short lessons

The first lesson asks why the government has three branches at all.

- 1. Why three branches?

The second tells who does what. Congress makes the laws, the President carries them out, and the courts decide what the laws mean.

- 2. Who does what?

The third asks what stops one branch from taking over, and shows how the branches keep each other in line.

- 3. What stops one branch?

Every new word is explained where it first appears.
