## "Not a revolutionary bill"

On October 3, 1965, at the foot of the Statue of Liberty, Lyndon Johnson signed the Hart-Celler Act and told the country not to worry: "This bill that we will sign today is not a revolutionary bill."

[beat]

The prediction failed completely. The United States now issues roughly a million green cards a year, and the foreign-born share of the population rose from 4.7 percent in 1970 to 14.3 percent by 2023.

- Foreign-born share, 1970: 4.7%
- By 2023: 14.3%

> Almost every immigration argument is about the machine that law built.

## Four doors

A green card comes through four doors. Guess which one is biggest.

[pause]

Family is the dominant door: about 65 percent of green cards over the past decade. Employment is about 16 percent. Refugees and asylees have averaged about 11, and the diversity lottery is the smallest door.

- Family: about 65%
- Employment: about 16%
- Refugees and asylees: about 11%

Spouses, unmarried minor children and parents of U.S. citizens face no numerical cap, and that single category is nearly half of all permanent immigration.

> Family reunification, not employment, is the system's center of gravity.

## The 7 percent rule

No country's nationals may receive more than 7 percent of the capped preference visas in a year. Iceland and India get the same allotment. So an Indian software engineer approved today faces a wait measured in decades.

- Same limit for India as for Iceland

The rule was written as a guarantee against discrimination. Its effect, two generations later, is a queue sorted by birthplace.

> Good intentions, mechanical consequences.

## Who is unauthorized

Pew's estimate put the unauthorized population at a record 14 million as of mid-2023. It is two populations, not one: millions settled for 18 years or more, and millions who arrived within the previous five.

- 14 million as of mid-2023
- Long-settled, and newly arrived

And a large minority never crossed a border unlawfully. Of the roughly 11 million people in the pre-surge population, about 42 percent entered legally on a visa and overstayed.

- Pre-surge: about 42% overstayed a visa

> "Unauthorized" is a legal category, not a behavioral one.

## Not yet an argument

Whether that machine admits too many people or too few, and what to do about the people already here, are contested questions on which serious people divide.

- Too many or too few: contested
