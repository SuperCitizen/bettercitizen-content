## Two numbers, no new law

In December 2023, Customs and Border Protection logged 302,034 encounters at the southwest border, the highest monthly total ever recorded. In September 2025, the Border Patrol made 8,386 apprehensions there.

- Dec. 2023: 302,034 encounters
- Sept. 2025: 8,386 apprehensions

Between those two numbers, not one word of the permanent immigration statute changed. The reversal was built from executive action, foreign-government cooperation, court fights, and one enormous appropriation.

> Policy is now made almost everywhere except where the Constitution puts lawmaking.

## What each number measures

An encounter counts an event at the border, not a person. One person crossing three times is three encounters. A removal is a formal deportation.

- Encounter: an event, not a person

Net migration is the only number that captures what the others miss, people quietly leaving or never coming, and it is estimated, not counted.

- Net migration: estimated, not counted

> Each number is real. Each is selected for its rhetorical job.

## Money can pass

The legislative event of this period was not an immigration bill. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act, signed July 4, 2025, was a budget reconciliation package, and inside it was roughly 170 billion dollars for immigration enforcement.

- About $170 billion for enforcement

Reconciliation needs 51 Senate votes, not 60. But it can only move money. New visa categories, legalization and asylum standards generally cannot survive its limits.

- 51 votes, but money only

> Enforcement can pass on party lines. Rebuilding the system needs a bipartisan vote.

## The same dashboard

Restrictionists point to the collapse in unlawful crossings as proof that enforcement, sufficiently resourced, works.

- Restrictionist: enforcement works

Expansionists point to 2025, the first year of net negative migration in roughly five decades by economists' estimate, and to projections of slower growth.

- Expansionist: slower growth projected

Centrists point to a test still running: with the border controlled, their theory predicts that legalization politics should now ease.

- Centrist: does control unlock legalization?

## Still open

As of mid-2026, the Supreme Court had heard the birthright citizenship case and not yet decided it. The lesson keeps a scorecard of which executive actions survived in court, follows three cases to watch, and marks what states can and cannot do.
