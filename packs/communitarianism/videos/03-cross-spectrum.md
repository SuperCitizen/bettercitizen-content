## Two bills, one justification

In February 2021, Mitt Romney, the Republican Party's 2012 presidential nominee, proposed sending American parents up to 350 dollars a month per young child. A month later, congressional Democrats passed a temporary child allowance of their own, 300 dollars a month for children under six, over unified Republican opposition.

- Romney: up to $350 a month
- Democrats: $300 a month, under six

[beat]

Two nearly identical policies, two opposed coalitions, and one justification available to both: families are institutions worth supporting as such.

> Families are institutions worth supporting as such. That justification is communitarian.

## A different overlap with each neighbor

Communitarianism borrows a different shared ground from each neighboring tradition. With conservatism, it shares the view that family, religious community and tradition matter. With progressivism, it shares support for universal provision and civic infrastructure. With socialism, it shares the argument that healthcare, education and basic housing should not be allocated by ability to pay.

- Conservatism: institutions and tradition
- Progressivism: universal provision
- Socialism: basic goods outside the market

That is why its adherents can land in either party, and why a communitarian framing can bridge left and right even when the coalitions refuse to merge.

## The deepest divide

The deep disagreement is with libertarianism, and even there it is more philosophical than total. Both prefer local, community-level decisions, and both worry about distant, unaccountable institutions.

- Shared: local decisions, civil society

What divides them is the starting point. Libertarians treat individual choice and property rights as foundational. Communitarians treat community membership as constitutive of selfhood.

- Libertarian: choice and property first
- Communitarian: membership first

> Choice as the foundation, or community as what makes the self.

## Left or right has no single answer

The three streams find different allies. The civic-republican stream works closest with mainstream progressivism, with a standing complaint that rights-language and policy wonkery are not a civic life. The religious-traditionalist stream is closest to traditionalist conservatism, while rejecting the free-market individualism of the older conservative coalition. The progressive stream is closest to social democracy, with universal programs and unions as its communitarian institutions.

- Civic-republican: nearest mainstream progressivism
- Religious-traditionalist: nearest traditionalist conservatism
- Progressive: nearest social democracy

The lesson has what divides communitarianism from each neighbor, the European equivalents, and five issues where the partisan alignment is misleading.
