## One diagnosis, three times

Seat Michael Sandel, R. R. Reno and Robert Reich on one panel. Ask whether American individualism has hollowed out shared life, and you will hear a single diagnosis three times over: atomization, civic decay, the unencumbered self.

- Shared diagnosis: atomization

[beat]

Then ask about same-sex marriage, school vouchers or immigration, and the consensus shatters.

> Three streams agree on the diagnosis and diverge on what it implies.

## One question, three answers

Put one question to all three streams. What does American civic life most need recovered?

[pause]

The civic-republican stream answers: democratic participation itself. A country can pass good laws and still hollow out, because participation is the good, not just a means to goods.

- Civic-republican: participation itself

The religious-traditionalist stream answers: the moral frameworks that make civic life possible. Participation is downstream of formation, and formation happens in families and religious communities.

- Religious-traditionalist: moral formation

The progressive stream answers: shared institutions everyone uses. Universal healthcare, public schools, transit, parks and unions are where shared identity is built in a diverse modern society.

- Progressive: institutions everyone uses

[check q3]

## Where they land

The streams diverge sharply on cultural questions, such as sexuality, religion and family, and on immigration. They converge on civic infrastructure, labor rights and most social-provision policies.

- Diverge: culture and immigration
- Converge: civic infrastructure, labor, social provision

One policy in the lesson's table draws strong support from all three: a universal child allowance. It strengthens families as institutions, and it is universal social provision, delivered through a shared civic commitment.

- Universal child allowance: strong support, all three

> The child allowance answers each stream's distinct priority at once.

[check q4]

## Rivals or allies

Each stream also has a standing complaint about the other two, and the lesson gives every one of them.

- Each stream faults the other two

Whether the three are rivals or allies in practice, the lesson does not settle. It has each stream's thinkers and key claims, the full table of twelve issues, and the coalitions that borrow from the shared core.
