## Where nothing was

In March 1958, on a bare lava field 11,000 feet up Mauna Loa volcano in Hawaii, a 29-year-old geochemist named Charles David Keeling switched on a new gas analyzer. He had picked the site because nothing was there: just clean mid-Pacific air. The first reading came back at 313 parts per million of carbon dioxide.

- 1958: 313 ppm

[beat]

The baseline has climbed every single year since. In 2025 the annual average was 427.

- 2025: 427 ppm

> Is the warming real? What should be done? Two different questions.

## What is settled

Three claims are as solidly established as anything in observational science. The planet has warmed: the long-term level stands at about 1.3 to 1.4 degrees Celsius above the 1850 to 1900 baseline. The cause is primarily human emission of greenhouse gases. And continued emissions will produce continued warming.

- Warming so far: about 1.3–1.4°C
- Cause: primarily human emissions
- Continued emissions, continued warming

None of these claims is contested in the peer-reviewed literature. And disagreement about climate policy does not require disagreeing with any of them.

> Disagreeing about climate policy does not require disagreeing with the science.

## A record year is not a warmer world

The World Meteorological Organization confirmed 2024 as the warmest year in the instrumental record, at about 1.55 degrees above that baseline. So has the Paris Agreement's 1.5 degree target been breached?

[pause]

No. A single year rides on top of natural variability, and an El Niño pushed 2024 well above the underlying trend. The Paris targets refer to the long-term warming level, averaged over decades.

- 2024 alone: about 1.55°C
- Long-term level: about 1.3–1.4°C

On current trajectories, the long-term level itself is likely to cross 1.5 degrees in the 2030s.

> One hot year does not breach the target. It previews the trend.

## What the science cannot answer

How much warming is acceptable is a values question, and the science cannot answer it. This pack carries three perspectives, and all three accept the science. The aggressive-mitigation advocate puts cutting emissions first. The adaptation-first advocate puts resilience first. The innovation-led centrist says what cuts emissions is technological substitution, so accelerate it.

- Aggressive mitigation
- Adaptation first
- Innovation led

The lesson has the Keeling Curve itself, the warming each emissions path would bring this century, and the Paris Agreement, which the United States has now left twice. The next lesson gives each of the three perspectives its strongest case.
