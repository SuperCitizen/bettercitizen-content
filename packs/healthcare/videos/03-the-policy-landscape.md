## The night the credits expired

On December 31, 2025, the enhanced premium tax credits that had financed a record 24.3 million marketplace enrollments expired on schedule. The average payment that enrollees actually make rose 58 percent, from 113 dollars a month to 178. And 1.2 million fewer people signed up for 2026 coverage.

- Payments up 58%
- 1.2 million fewer sign-ups

> Not a stalemate but a collision: expansion against retrenchment.

## The 2025 law

The summer before, on July 4, 2025, the One Big Beautiful Bill Act was signed. The Congressional Budget Office's score: about 1.06 trillion dollars less federal spending on Medicaid and the marketplaces over ten years, and about 10 million more people without health insurance in 2034.

- $1.06 trillion less federal spending
- About 10 million more uninsured in 2034

The largest single line is a work requirement for adults in the Medicaid expansion group: 80 hours a month, documented. It is scored at 326 billion dollars.

- Work requirement: $326 billion

Arkansas ran that experiment in 2018. About 18,000 adults lost coverage in seven months. Researchers found that more than 95 percent of the people targeted already met the requirement or qualified for an exemption, that employment did not measurably increase, and that the losses came overwhelmingly from reporting failures.

- Arkansas 2018: about 18,000 lost coverage

## 51 votes and 60

That law passed the Senate through budget reconciliation, which needs only a simple majority and carries only budget items. Extending the credits is a regular statute, and in practice it needs 60 Senate votes. In December the three-year extension drew 51.

- Reconciliation: a simple majority
- A regular statute: 60 votes in practice

> Money can be moved by 51 votes. Rules and rescues need 60.

## Three readings of the law

The market reformer sees overdue discipline: a work expectation for able-bodied adults, and a program refocused on the disabled, children and the elderly poor.

- Market reformer: overdue discipline

The single-payer advocate sees the Arkansas mechanism nationalized: coverage removed by administrative friction, not by an honest vote to cut benefits.

- Single-payer advocate: cuts by friction

The ACA incrementalist sees the framework being defunded rather than completed: every dollar comes out of the coverage architecture that strategy was counting on.

- Incrementalist: defunded, not completed

## What nobody knows yet

Will the Senate extend the credits in any form? Will national work requirements repeat Arkansas's paperwork losses, or will implementation learn? Nobody knows these answers yet.

> The 2026 to 2028 calendar will supply the evidence.
