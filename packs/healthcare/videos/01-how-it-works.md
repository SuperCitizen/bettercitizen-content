## One operation, two prices

In 2009, 19,368 adults in California were hospitalized for appendicitis. Same state, same year, uncomplicated cases only. The lowest hospital charge was 1,529 dollars. The highest was 182,955 dollars.

- Lowest charge: $1,529
- Highest charge: $182,955

No patient could have learned the price in advance, and almost none of them paid it directly.

## An accident of war

No one designed American healthcare. In 1942, Congress froze wages. Employers competing for scarce workers could not offer raises, so they offered benefits. In 1943 the IRS ruled that an employer's contributions to a group health plan were not taxable income to the worker. In 1954 Congress made that permanent.

- 1942: wages frozen
- 1943: employer premiums untaxed
- 1954: made permanent

> Insurance that comes from your job is a side effect of World War Two.

## A patchwork

In 1965, Medicare and Medicaid were added for the two groups the employer system left out: retirees and the poor. Today about 165 million people under 65 are covered through an employer. About 69 million are in Medicare, and 75.7 million in Medicaid and the children's program, CHIP. And 26.7 million people under 65 have no insurance at all.

- Employer: about 165 million
- Medicare: about 69 million
- Medicaid and CHIP: 75.7 million
- Uninsured: 26.7 million

> There is no single system here. It is a patchwork.

## It's the prices

The United States spends 14,775 dollars per person on health. The average in comparable wealthy countries is 7,860. The most-cited answer in the research is the title of a famous paper: "It's Still the Prices, Stupid." Americans do not visit doctors or occupy hospital beds more than their peers. They pay more per unit.

- United States: $14,775 per person
- Peer average: $7,860

> Americans do not use more care. They pay more for each unit.

## Whose income?

Prices are incomes. Every excess dollar is somebody's revenue: a hospital system's, a physician's, a drugmaker's, an insurer's. And what causes the gap is contested: fragmentation, the absence of real markets, or fixable seams in the Affordable Care Act.

- Every excess dollar is somebody's revenue
- The cause is contested
