## Sixteen days, forty-six years

Cincinnatus, handed absolute power by Rome, resigned it on the sixteenth day. The oldest national emergency in the United States, declared in November 1979, turned forty-six in November 2025, and was renewed again.

- Cincinnatus: 16 days
- Oldest U.S. emergency: 46 years

Three schools study the same record, and each finds the binding constraint on emergency power in a different place.

> Three theories of where the binding constraint lives.

## Executive flexibility

Eric Posner and Adrian Vermeule argue that in crises the Madisonian separation of powers does not and cannot govern. What disciplines the modern executive, on this view, is politics: elections, opinion, markets, and a bureaucracy that leaks and resists.

- Posner and Vermeule, The Executive Unbound

They point to 2025 and 2026. The tariffs were abandoned, they argue, because markets and electoral arithmetic made them unsustainable, and the court rulings ratified a political verdict already rendered.

> The flexibility case: politics, not law, disciplines the executive.

## Legalist reform

The reform school argues that the default decides. Its flagship, the ARTICLE ONE Act, would have every declared emergency expire after 30 days unless Congress affirmatively approves it. Today it takes two-thirds of both chambers to stop an emergency. Under the Act it would take simple majorities to continue one.

- ARTICLE ONE Act: expires after 30 days
- Simple majorities to continue

They point to 2025 and 2026 as well. The executive complied with adverse court orders, they argue, which means legal form is doing real work.

> The reform case: change the default, and inaction switches sides.

## Judicial engagement

The third school argues that the decisive variable is judicial nerve. Its charter is Sterling v. Constantin, from 1932, when the governor of Texas declared martial law to shut down oil wells. The Supreme Court held that the limits of military discretion, and whether they have been overstepped, "are judicial questions." An emergency declaration is not self-validating.

- Sterling v. Constantin, 1932
- A declaration is not self-validating

They point to 2025 and 2026 too. Courts struck down the tariff program and enjoined Guard deployments, they argue, and the troops demobilized.

> The engagement case: courts can police an emergency while it matters.

## What the record cannot separate

Each school reads the same record as vindication. The lesson does not settle where the binding constraint lives: 2025 and 2026 showed law, politics and courts all binding together, with no controlled experiment separating them.

- Law, politics and courts, binding together

The lesson gives each school its case at full strength and its own concession, runs the September 11 authorization through all three, and sets out what evidence would vindicate or wound each.
