## An emergency since 1933

In 1973 a special Senate committee set out to answer a question nobody in government could: how many states of emergency was the United States under? Its report opens: "Since March 9, 1933, the United States has been in a state of declared national emergency."

[beat]

Not one emergency. Four, stacked and forgotten, with some 470 statutory provisions that switched on whenever a President declared one.

- 4 standing emergencies
- Some 470 provisions

> Four decades of statute, switched on and never off.

## The machine and its safety valve

The National Emergencies Act of 1976 did not define an emergency. It imposed procedure: declare, specify the powers, renew each year, and Congress can end it.

- Declare, specify, renew, terminate

The last step was the keystone. Congress could recall an emergency by concurrent resolution: a vote of the two chambers that never reaches the President's desk, so he could not veto it.

- The recall: a majority vote, no veto

## Chadha breaks the machine

In 1983, in INS v. Chadha, a case about one deportable foreign student, the Supreme Court held legislative vetoes unconstitutional. Congress patched the act in 1985 with a joint resolution, which the President signs or vetoes. To end an emergency the President wants to keep, Congress must now override his veto.

- 1983: legislative vetoes void
- 1985: a resolution he can veto

> A majority-vote recall now takes two-thirds of both chambers.

By the Brennan Center's tally, updated in May 2026, 48 national emergencies are in effect. The oldest was declared by Jimmy Carter in November 1979.

- 48 emergencies in effect

## Courts reading two words

In 2025 the administration used a sanctions statute, IEEPA, to impose tariffs. No president had done that before. On February 20, 2026, the Supreme Court held, six to three, that the statute's power to regulate importation does not include tariffs.

The holding was statutory, not constitutional. Tariffs under express delegations survived. So did the underlying emergency declarations.

- A statutory holding, not constitutional
- The declarations stayed in effect

> Courts can strike the action, but not the emergency.

## Broken, or convenient?

The lesson leaves its largest question open: is this a system that is broken, or one that every branch, Congress included, finds convenient to leave as it is?

- Broken, or convenient to every branch?
