## One sentence, and an arrest

The Constitution gives emergencies one sentence, the Suspension Clause. The republic has been arguing about the rest since 1861, when soldiers arrested John Merryman at two o'clock in the morning, the Chief Justice ruled the President's conduct unconstitutional, and the President declined to obey.

> The Constitution gives emergencies one sentence.

## Three lessons

The first reads that sentence, and then the wartime cases, with the opinions quoted and argued with: Merryman, Milligan, Quirin, Korematsu and Hamdi.

- 1. The constitution of emergency

The second maps what Congress built instead: the National Emergencies Act, IEEPA and the 2026 tariff ruling, the Insurrection Act, and Youngstown's three categories.

- 2. The statutory emergency state

The third holds the argument open among three schools: executive flexibility, legalist reform and judicial engagement. Each gets its case at full strength.

- 3. Reform and the permanent emergency

## Where to start

As of mid-2026, forty-eight national emergencies are running, and the oldest is in its fifth decade. Start with the first lesson, The constitution of emergency. It begins at two o'clock in the morning.
