## A quarter, or a penny?

In early 2025, pollsters asked Americans what share of the federal budget goes to foreign aid. The average answer was about a quarter. The real figure is roughly one percent: in fiscal 2025 the entire international affairs budget came to 45 billion dollars, out of 7,010 billion in spending.

- Average guess: 26%
- Actual: about 1%

People who believe the first number will support a plan that cannot work.

> Cut the aid, balance the books: arithmetically impossible.

## Three channels

Federal spending flows through three channels. Mandatory spending, about 4.2 trillion dollars, is written into permanent law: Social Security, Medicare, Medicaid. The law itself defines who qualifies and what they get, and no annual vote occurs.

- Mandatory: about $4.2 trillion, no annual vote

Discretionary spending, roughly 1.8 trillion dollars, is the part Congress must appropriate every single year. About half of it is defense.

- Discretionary: roughly $1.8 trillion, voted yearly

Net interest, 970 billion dollars, is the price of past borrowing.

- Net interest: $970 billion

> Most of the budget is never voted on.

## An insurance company with an army

Social Security is the single largest federal activity, at 23 percent of spending. Medicare is 14 percent. Other health programs, mostly Medicaid, are another 14. Defense is 13. And net interest is 14: since fiscal 2024, the government has spent more on interest than on national defense.

- Social Security: 23%
- Medicare: 14%
- Net interest: 14%
- Defense: 13%

> In dollar terms: an insurance company with an army.

## On credit

Mandatory spending plus interest came to about 5.2 trillion dollars, almost exactly what the government collected in total revenue. So everything Congress debated and voted on that year was, in an accounting sense, financed by borrowing.

- Mandatory + interest: about all revenue

The deficit was about 1.8 trillion dollars. Eliminating all of defense would not have balanced the budget.

- Deficit: about $1.8 trillion

> The annual budget fight covers a minority of the budget, on credit.

## What the map does not settle

This is composition, not judgment. It does not establish that the deficit is a crisis, or that it is not.
