## The third collapse in one year

On April 30, 2026, the President signed the bill that reopened the Department of Homeland Security, ending a 76-day lapse. It was the third funding collapse of a single fiscal year.

- 3 funding lapses in fiscal 2026

The diagram every textbook prints shows the President proposing in February and Congress passing twelve appropriations bills by October 1.

> That process has run on schedule four times since 1977.

## The machine that actually runs

When the date is missed, Congress reaches for a continuing resolution, which extends the prior year's funding levels for weeks or months and bars new starts. When the bills finally move, leadership bundles them into an omnibus. And when nothing passes, agencies must cease operations: a shutdown.

- Continuing resolution: last year's levels
- Omnibus: the bills, bundled
- Shutdown: no funding passed

Funding requires affirmative action by three veto players: the House, the Senate and the President.

> Any one of three veto players can turn the deadline into leverage.

## A limit on paying, not on spending

The debt limit is a cap on the total debt the Treasury may have outstanding. It does not authorize or restrain any spending. Every dollar that requires borrowing was already enacted by past Congresses.

- Debt limit: caps borrowing, not spending

> Refusing to raise it is refusing to pay for dinner already eaten.

## Must the President spend it?

Once Congress appropriates, must the President spend? A 1974 law says yes, unless Congress approves a proposed rescission within 45 days.

- Rescission: Congress has 45 days

On August 28, 2025, the administration sent a rescission message so close to September 30 that 4.9 billion dollars in foreign-aid funds would expire before the clock ran. That is a pocket rescission: spending canceled without congressional approval.

- Pocket rescission: the funds expire first

GAO's position is that pocket rescissions are illegal. The administration's position is that the 1974 law unconstitutionally invades executive authority. As of mid-2026, the fight remains unresolved on the merits.

- GAO: illegal
- Administration: the law is unconstitutional

> Does the power of the purse describe the Constitution, or a custom?

## Process is power

How to read all this depends partly on the previous lesson's camps. A hawk sees institutions doing what unconstrained incentives predict, and wants harder rules. A dove sees procedural fetishism distracting from outcomes. An investment advocate sees a process that cannot distinguish what borrowing buys.

- Hawk: harder rules
- Dove: outcomes over procedure
- Investment advocate: what borrowing buys
