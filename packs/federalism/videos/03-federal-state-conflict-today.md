## Forbidden to help, free to defy

In 2010 Arizona passed a law meant to help enforce federal immigration law. The federal government sued, and the Supreme Court struck down three of the law's four challenged provisions.

- Arizona: helping, struck down

Meanwhile, states have legalized a drug that federal law prohibits, and no court has struck those laws down.

- Marijuana states: defying, still standing

> A state can be forbidden to help, and free to defy?

## What "contrary" means

Valid federal law beats contrary state law. Preemption is the set of rules courts use to decide what contrary means.

Sometimes Congress writes a clause that displaces state law. Sometimes federal regulation is so pervasive that it leaves no room for states. That is part of what sank Arizona: immigration was Washington's field. And sometimes a state law conflicts with a federal one, because nobody can obey both, or because it stands as an obstacle to what Congress intended.

- Express: Congress wrote a clause
- Field: no room for states
- Conflict: impossible, or an obstacle

## Why legalization stands

Preemption displaces a state law that conflicts with federal law. A state that repeals its own penalties for marijuana has not enacted a conflicting law. It has enacted nothing.

- Repeal: no conflicting law

And Washington cannot order a state legislature to keep prohibition on the books. That would commandeer it. The federal ban is still valid, and federal agents may enforce it. They have nowhere near the personnel to do that in fifty states without state help.

- No commandeering state legislatures
- The federal ban: still valid

> Washington wins the collisions it chooses to have.

## Withhold, but never nullify

Sanctuary jurisdictions use the same shield. They limit their own participation in federal immigration enforcement. Second Amendment sanctuaries do the same with federal gun laws.

- Sanctuary jurisdictions: immigration
- Second Amendment sanctuaries: guns

There is a line. Missouri passed a law declaring various federal gun laws "invalid to this state," and penalizing local police for helping enforce them. A federal court struck it down.

- Missouri: struck down

> A state may withhold its own hands. It may not nullify federal law.

Whether uniformity or divergence should prevail is a real argument. The lesson gives three answers their best case. The nationalist calls fifty rules a tax on everything that moves.
