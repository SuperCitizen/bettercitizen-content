## The law that does not exist

The drinking age in every state is 21, and no federal law says it must be. The closest thing is an instruction: withhold a percentage of federal highway money from any state that lets people under 21 buy alcohol.

- Drinking age: 21 in every state
- No federal law requires it

South Dakota sued, and lost, in South Dakota v. Dole, in 1987. Then it raised its drinking age like everyone else.

> Nobody was ordered to do anything.

## By contract, not by command

Congress cannot order states to act. So it pays them. Federal grants to state and local governments run over 1 trillion dollars a year, and Medicaid alone is the majority of it.

- Grants: over $1 trillion a year

In fiscal 2024, federal money was 34.2 percent of state revenue across all fifty states. Even the least reliant state, North Dakota, raised nearly a quarter of its revenue from Washington.

- 34.2% of state revenue

> States are free to refuse sums no state can actually refuse.

## What strings are allowed

The Dole case set four requirements for a string. The spending must serve the general welfare. The condition must be unambiguous: states must know the deal when they take the money. It must be related to the federal interest in the program. And it may not induce states to violate some other part of the Constitution.

- General welfare
- Unambiguous
- Related to the program
- No constitutional violation

## Where a string becomes a leash

In 2012, in NFIB v. Sebelius, the Supreme Court met a deal designed so that no state could walk away. The Affordable Care Act expanded Medicaid, and a state that refused could lose all of its existing Medicaid funding.

In Dole, South Dakota stood to lose less than half of one percent of its budget. Here the money was more than 10 percent of most states' entire budgets.

- Dole: under half of 1%
- NFIB: over 10% of state budgets

Chief Justice Roberts called the threat "a gun to the head." Seven justices agreed that pressure had turned into compulsion.

> "A gun to the head": pressure had turned into compulsion.

[check q3-coercion-transfer]

## The blank space

The Court marked one point and declined to draw the rest of the line. Where compulsion begins, between half a percent and ten percent, no court has said.

- Half a percent to 10%: unmarked
